## Bottom Line

Adult-learning theory is not one theory but three families answering three different questions: *who controls the learning* (autonomy), *how experience becomes learning* (experience/reflection), and *where learning lives* (the individual mind vs. the community). None is a validated theory in the strict sense; all are best treated as assumption-sets and design heuristics. They share one well-evidenced spine that is stronger than any of them individually: **competence and autonomy are achievements, not starting conditions — so structure comes first and fades as the learner grows, and the most reliable engine of that growth is guided practice with feedback inside a real community of work.** Read that way, the families stop competing and start composing.

---

## The single thread running through all three phases

Each family arrives, by its own route, at the same caution about novices:

- **Phase 1 (autonomy)** says self-direction backfires for beginners — the expertise-reversal effect and cognitive load mean novices need guidance they cannot yet supply themselves. Autonomy is the *destination*, staged toward (Grow), not the *starting method*.
- **Phase 2 (experience)** says, with Dewey, that *not all experience is educative*. Raw experience and unstructured reflection fail novices; experience becomes learning only through skilled facilitation and scaffolded reflection.
- **Phase 3 (social)** says newcomers learn safely at the **periphery** — legitimate but low-stakes participation, supported by old-timers — which is simply *social scaffolding* by another name.

Three vocabularies, one finding: **scaffold the novice, fade toward autonomy.** This is the most defensible, cross-validated claim in all of Part I, and it should anchor every application decision below.

## Where they conflict (and how to hold the conflict)

These families are mostly complementary, but three genuine tensions are worth naming rather than papering over:

**1. Acquisition vs. participation.** The strong situated-learning claim (knowledge can't be decontextualized at all) genuinely conflicts with the acquisition view underlying andragogy and most of experiential theory. Sfard's resolution — both metaphors are needed, and committing to only one is dangerous — is the position the evidence supports. A resident needs pharmacology *acquired* and clinician-identity *participated into*.

**2. Autonomy as premise vs. autonomy as achievement.** Knowles's self-concept assumption treats adult self-direction as a near-given; the cognitive-science evidence treats it as something earned per domain. When these collide, the evidence sides with *achievement*: an experienced adult can still be a rank novice in a new domain (Grow's situational point), and should be taught accordingly.

**3. Rational vs. whole-person transformation.** Mezirow's rationalist, critical-reflection account conflicts with the Boyd/Dirkx depth-psychology and Freire social-emancipatory strands over whether deep change is primarily cognitive or also emotional, imaginal, relational, and political. For identity and faith contexts, the whole-person strands fit better; for procedural reframing, the rational account suffices.

The practical move in all three cases is the same: **don't pick a winner; pick the lens that fits the learner's stage and the content's nature.**

## The Unified Decision Guide

Work top to bottom. The first honest answer routes you.

**Step 1 — Diagnose the learner's stage *in this domain* (not their age).**
Use Grow's readiness frame, per domain:
- **S1 Dependent / true novice** → directive instruction, worked examples, modeling, immediate feedback. *Do not* hand a novice autonomy, an unstructured "experience," or a sink-or-swim community role. (Phase 1 + Phase 2's Dewey caveat.)
- **S2 Interested** → guided practice; sell the "why" (andragogy's need-to-know); structured reflection with explicit prompts.
- **S3 Involved** → facilitation; real problems; peer learning; the learner co-owns goals.
- **S4 Self-directed** → consult/delegate; self-determined goals (heutagogy); open exploration.

**Step 2 — Match the family to the goal.**
- Goal is *motivating capable adults / individualized paths* → **autonomy family** (andragogy, SDL; heutagogy only at S4 with demonstrated capability).
- Goal is *turning real experience into durable learning, or reshaping assumptions/identity* → **experiential/reflective/transformative family** (cycle + structured debrief; transformative *only* with psychological safety and ethical care).
- Goal is *onboarding, craft mastery, or professional belonging* → **social family** (LPP/apprenticeship as default; CoP for cross-team knowledge; action learning for real-problem leadership development).

**Step 3 — Always preserve the non-negotiables.**
Whatever family you choose: keep a **skilled facilitator/mentor/old-timer** in the loop; build in **feedback**; **scaffold then fade**; and combine **programmed knowledge (P) with questioning/participation (Q)**.

**Step 4 — Honor the ethical and evidentiary limits.**
- Don't manufacture traumatic "disorienting dilemmas," especially where you hold authority (teacher, clergy, supervisor).
- Don't assess reflection in a way that turns it into compliance theater.
- Don't assume a *designed* community or action-learning set works because participants enjoyed it — measure behavior/practice change, not satisfaction.
- Don't use learning-styles labels at all.

## Consolidated Application by Context

### Clinical / Training
The three families nest cleanly onto the clinical training arc. **Autonomy:** reserve SDL and individualized learning plans for senior trainees and practitioners (Murad: advanced learners benefit more; SDL helps knowledge more than skills); scaffold juniors. **Experience:** simulation + *guided debrief* is the flagship — the large effects of simulation (Cook 2011) depend on the quality of the facilitated debrief, not the scenario; scripted debriefing helps (Cheng 2013); treat reflective portfolios as developmental, not pure assessment. **Social:** residency *is* legitimate peripheral participation — bounded responsibility progressing to independence under supervision; use CoPs for QI and CPD but evaluate any deliberately cultivated CoP with real outcome measures (Ranmuthugala: attribution is hard); action-learning sets are defensible for clinical leadership with eyes open on ROI. **Net move:** direct instruction + supervised deliberate practice for novices → faded autonomy + self-directed CPD for the experienced, all inside the clinical community.

### Ministry / Teaching
**Autonomy:** match teaching style to readiness — catechesis and foundational doctrine for newcomers benefit from structured, authoritative teaching; mature members thrive in self-chosen study, covenants, and lay-led ministry; watch for the S1-learner/S4-teacher mismatch that sinks volunteer-led small groups that assume everyone is ready to self-direct. **Experience/transformative:** faith formation maps onto the cycle and resonates powerfully with conversion/transformation language — which is exactly why *ethical care* is paramount given the authority gradient; favor relational mentoring (Daloz: support + challenge) and voluntary discourse over engineered crisis. **Social:** small groups, discipleship, and catechesis *are* communities of practice; the historic master-apprentice model of discipleship is LPP avant la lettre — give newcomers legitimate, depended-upon roles and old-timer mentorship rather than information transmission. **Net move:** apprenticeship + legitimate roles + supported (never forced) reflection.

### Creative / Content
**Autonomy:** self-planned learning projects and self-determined goals (heutagogy) are where creative voice flourishes — *but only once* foundations and feedback loops exist; beginners left to pure self-direction ingrain bad habits. **Experience:** craft is learned through make → critique → reframe → remake loops (Kolb cycle; Schön's studio "reflective conversation with the situation"); use deliberate practice for technical sub-skills but protect *exploratory* practice for voice; major creative breakthroughs are often emotionally driven reframings (Boyd/Dirkx fits better than rational Mezirow). **Social:** craft has always been learned by participation — guilds, ateliers, scenes, and today's online creator communities (Discord, forums, critique groups) as living cases of LPP; the developmental sequence (pedagogy → andragogy → heutagogy) is well illustrated by makers moving from structured tuition to peer learning to treating the medium itself as an instrument. **Net move:** join an active critique-based community and contribute real work early, scaffolded by modeling and feedback, fading to self-determined exploration.

## Quick-Reference Heuristics

- **Age is not stage.** Diagnose readiness per domain, every time.
- **Structure first, fade to autonomy.** The whole of Part I in five words.
- **Not all experience is educative** (Dewey). Design the experience and the reflection, or skip both.
- **The facilitator is the lever.** Across all three families, skilled guidance is the highest-leverage investment.
- **P + Q, acquisition + participation.** Never let enthusiasm for one crowd out the other.
- **Measure change, not smiles.** Especially for designed CoPs and action learning.
- **Drop learning styles.** Keep the cycle.
- **Mind the authority gradient.** Don't engineer disorienting dilemmas from a position of power.

## When to Reach for Part I

Reach for this Part when the question is *how people learn and how to structure a learning experience for adults* — designing a curriculum, an onboarding path, a training kit, a small-group arc, a mentorship, or a self-development plan. Reach for **Phase 1** when the live issue is control and motivation; **Phase 2** when it is turning experience into learning or reshaping how someone sees; **Phase 3** when it is onboarding, belonging, or learning a craft among others. Reach for **Part II (cognitive science)** when you need the mechanism *underneath* these theories — why scaffolding works, how memory and load actually behave — and for **Part III (instructional design)** when you need to turn any of this into a built artifact.
