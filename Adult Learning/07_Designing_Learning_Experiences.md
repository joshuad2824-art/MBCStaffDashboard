## Bottom Line

Start from the desired outcome and what learners must DO, not the content to cover — this single principle unites backward design (Wiggins & McTighe), action mapping (Cathy Moore), and performance consulting (Mager & Pipe, Gilbert), and it implies the most important design decision often comes before any course is built: frequently the right answer is a job aid or performance-support tool, not training at all. The field offers two distinct evidence tiers: objective-setting and process frameworks (Bloom, ADDIE, Mager's ABCD) are largely committee consensus or practitioner heuristics with weak experimental grounding, whereas a smaller set — Gagné's events, scaffolding/fading, 4C/ID, Merrill's First Principles, cognitive-load-based chunking — has stronger research support. This phase operationalizes the earlier library: "structure-first/fade-to-autonomy" becomes scaffolding-and-fading and 4C/ID; cognitive load underlies chunking; durable-learning techniques should be designed in, not bolted on.

---

## Core Principles

- **Design backward from what learners must DO** — outcome and assessment before activities and content.
- **Consider a job aid before a course.** Knowledge can live in the head (training) or in the world (performance support); often the latter is right.
- **Two evidence tiers.** Process frameworks are practitioner heuristics; Gagné, scaffolding, 4C/ID, and Merrill are better grounded.
- **Whole-task first for complex skills.** Don't fragment a complex skill into isolated subskills — the hardest part is coordinating them (4C/ID).
- **Scaffold and fade.** Effective scaffolding is contingent, gradually faded, and transfers responsibility — the operational form of "structure first, fade to autonomy."

## Deep Dive

### Learning Objectives and Taxonomies

**Bloom's original (1956)** divided the cognitive domain into six levels (Knowledge, Comprehension, Application, Analysis, Synthesis, Evaluation), with companion affective (Krathwohl, Bloom & Masia 1964) and psychomotor (Simpson, Dave, Harrow) domains. **Crucial caveat:** it was a committee consensus for classifying exam items, not a validated theory — "the cumulative hierarchy model of the original taxonomy did not have a strong basis in evidence" (*Probing Internal Assumptions of the Revised Bloom's Taxonomy*, 2022). **Anderson & Krathwohl (2001)** changed nouns to verbs (Remember → Create), put Create at the apex, and added a **Knowledge dimension** (factual, conceptual, procedural, metacognitive), producing a two-dimensional Taxonomy Table and relaxing the hierarchy.

**Mager** (*Preparing Instructional Objectives*, 1962) required objectives to specify **Performance, Condition, Criterion** (popularly expanded to **ABCD**: Audience, Behavior, Condition, Degree), insisting on observable verbs over "fuzzy" ones (know, understand). **Eisner's critique (1967):** pre-specifying all outcomes behaviorally can't capture emergent, valuable results; he proposed **expressive objectives/outcomes**.

**Other taxonomies:** **Fink** (2003) — six *non-hierarchical, relational* categories (Foundational Knowledge, Application, Integration, Human Dimension, Caring, Learning How to Learn), synergistic, including affective/metacognitive aims; widely cited but "unclear how and to what extent the taxonomy is being adopted" (Arend et al. scoping review, *IJAD* 2026, 141 manuscripts 2003–2024). **SOLO** (Biggs & Collis 1982) — five levels rating the *structure of an observed response* (Prestructural → Extended Abstract). **Marzano & Kendall** (2001/2007) — crosses six processing levels with three knowledge domains, foregrounding the Self-System (will the learner engage?) and Metacognitive System.

### Backward Design / Understanding by Design

Wiggins & McTighe's *Understanding by Design* (1998/2005) formalizes **three stages**: identify desired results → determine acceptable evidence → plan learning experiences. Its diagnostic centerpiece is the **"twin sins"**: activity-oriented design (engaging activities, no payoff) and coverage-oriented design (marching through content). Supporting constructs: essential questions, enduring understandings, transfer as the goal, **WHERETO**, and the **six facets of understanding** (explanation, interpretation, application, perspective, empathy, self-knowledge). Its lineage runs to **Ralph Tyler's** *Basic Principles* (1949) and the four "Tyler rationale" questions — the origin of outcomes-first thinking. UbD is disciplined practitioner wisdom, not an experimentally validated intervention.

### Instructional Design Process Models

**ADDIE** (Analysis, Design, Development, Implementation, Evaluation) is best described as a **generic umbrella**, not an authored model. The origin story credits Florida State University (1975) for the U.S. Army (IPISD), but Molenda's "In Search of the Elusive ADDIE Model" (*Performance Improvement*, 2003/2015) found no one who claimed to have invented it — "the label seems not to have a single author... just an umbrella term." Its 1975 form was waterfall; practitioners made it iterative. Critiques: too linear, slow, defers testing.

**SAM** (Successive Approximation Model; Allen with Sites, *Leaving ADDIE for SAM*, 2012) is the agile, rapid-prototyping answer: a brief **Preparation** phase, an **Iterative Design** phase opening with the **"Savvy Start"** (brainstorm producing ≥3 rough prototypes), and **Iterative Development** (Alpha/Beta/Gold). Allen concedes that if ADDIE works, keep it.

**Dick & Carey** (*The Systematic Design of Instruction*, 1978; later eds.) is the canonical systems-approach model — ~9–10 interacting components with tight objective–assessment–strategy alignment. **Design Thinking** (empathize/define/ideate/prototype/test) and **Agile/LLAMA** import iteration from software/product practice — practice frameworks, not validated theories.

**Gagné's Nine Events** (*The Conditions of Learning*, 1965): gain attention, inform of objective, stimulate recall, present content, provide guidance, elicit performance, provide feedback, assess, enhance retention/transfer. Each maps to an internal cognitive process. Underneath sits his more radical claim — the **five categories of learning outcomes** (verbal information, intellectual skills, cognitive strategies, attitudes, motor skills), each needing different conditions. The most research-grounded process framework.

### Sequencing, Scaffolding, and Chunking

**Vygotsky's ZPD** (the gap between solo and assisted performance) and **scaffolding** are not the same — **Vygotsky never used "scaffolding"**; it was coined by **Wood, Bruner & Ross (1976)** describing six tutoring functions. ZPD is *diagnostic*; scaffolding is *design*. Effective scaffolding has contingent support, **gradual fading**, and transfer of responsibility — the direct operational form of "structure first, fade to autonomy" and the worked-example/guidance-fading effects from Phase 05. Empirical caveat: dynamic-assessment evidence is mixed (Caffrey, Fuchs & Fuchs 2008 — DA and traditional tests "similarly predicted future academic performance," with superior validity only for non-contingent feedback and students with disabilities).

**Elaboration Theory** (Reigeluth, 1979–83): macro-level simple-to-complex sequencing via an **epitome** and the **"zoom lens"** metaphor. **Merrill's First Principles** (2002, *ETR&D*): learning is promoted when it is **problem/task-centered** and cycles through **activation, demonstration, application, integration** — a research synthesis, with the Pebble-in-the-Pond design method. **van Merriënboer's 4C/ID** (*Ten Steps to Complex Learning*, 2007) is the most rigorous complex-learning model, built explicitly on CLT: four components — **learning tasks** (authentic, whole-task, simple-to-complex with fading scaffolds), **supportive information** (the "theory"), **procedural/just-in-time information** (faded as proficiency grows), and **part-task practice** (drill only to automate). Whole-task first. A *Frontiers in Education* systematic review (Sept 2025; 55 studies, PRISMA) found "many studies referencing the 4C/ID model lack detailed descriptions of its implementation," even as its multilevel meta-analysis found **a moderate positive effect (g = 0.76, SE = 0.31, p = 0.014)**.

### Microlearning, Performance Support, and "When NOT to Build a Course"

**Microlearning** has reasonable evidence for discrete knowledge, procedural skills, retention, and engagement, but is poorly suited to **complex, integrated skills and higher-order thinking** and can create an "illusion of learning"; use within a blended approach.

**Performance support / EPSS.** Gery's *Electronic Performance Support Systems* (1991): deliver help at the moment of need, within the workflow. **Rossett** systematized job aids, distinguishing **planners** (before/after a task) from **sidekicks** (during). The central decision: knowledge in the head (training) vs. in the world (job aid/EPSS) — put it in the world when the task is infrequent, complex-but-rule-based, frequently changing, high-consequence, or look-up-able.

**Performance consulting.** Mager & Pipe's *Analyzing Performance Problems* (1970) supplies the acid test: **"If the person's life depended on it, could they do it?"** If yes, it's not a training problem (it's motivation, incentives, tools, environment) and a course will fail. **Gilbert's** Behavior Engineering Model (*Human Competence*, 1978) locates most deficits in the **environment** (information, resources, incentives), usually tracing to management. **Cathy Moore's Action Mapping** (*Map It*, 2017) — "a mashup of performance consulting and backward design": identify a measurable business goal → what people need to DO (and why they aren't) → realistic practice → minimum information (often a job aid). Her thesis: "a 'course' is rarely the solution." She's candid it maps poorly onto K-12/higher-ed where there are no specific on-the-job behaviors.

## Evidence Base & Open Debates

- **Two clear tiers.** Practitioner heuristics with weak experimental grounding: ADDIE, UbD, SAM, action mapping, most objective-writing formulas, Bloom's hierarchy. Better-grounded: Gagné's events, scaffolding/fading, cognitive-load-based chunking, 4C/ID (g = 0.76), Merrill's synthesis.
- **Bloom was consensus, not evidence,** and its hierarchy is not strictly cumulative.
- **Model proliferation and the model-practice gap:** dozens of overlapping models; experts work more fluidly than any model prescribes; 4C/ID fidelity varies widely (2025 review).
- **Behavioral-objectives critique stands** (Eisner) — especially in creative, affective, and discipleship contexts.
- **Over-engineering risk:** heavy models (4C/ID, Dick & Carey) waste resources on simple needs.
- **Reproducibility gaps:** microlearning and performance-support evidence skews to vendor/small-study sources.
- **Disputed histories:** ADDIE's origin/authorship is genuinely contested (Molenda).

## Application by Context

### Clinical / Training
Medicine has moved to **competency-based medical education**: ACGME/ABMS six core competencies (1999), ACGME **Milestones** (2013; Nasca et al., *NEJM* 2012), **CanMEDS** (1996/2005/2015), and **EPAs** (ten Cate 2005, *Medical Education* — "a unit of professional practice... entrusted to unsupervised execution once sufficient competence is attained"). EPAs are backward design applied to clinical work. **CME** (ACCME) requires a documented **professional practice gap** and outcome-appropriate formats — institutionalized backward design. For **EHR training**, the train-vs-job-aid decision is decisive: KLAS Arch Collaborative data find "the single greatest predictor of user experience is... how users rate the quality of the EHR-specific training they received," with the most satisfied clinicians receiving **11+ hours** of onboarding (recommended minimum ~3 hours). Yet the **tip sheet/job aid** is the most cost-effective tool for the constant stream of incremental system changes. Build role-based training for foundational workflows; use job aids for infrequent or changing tasks. For self-service kits, build a course only where a genuine in-the-head skill gap exists; otherwise build a tip sheet.

### Ministry / Teaching
Backward design is the natural lesson/curriculum engine: define the enduring understanding (or, for a sermon series, the transformation sought), the evidence learners "got it," then activities — avoiding the twin sins of activity-driven crafts and coverage-driven content marches. Gagné's nine events make a clean lesson/sermon skeleton. Fink's taxonomy is apt because it legitimizes **Caring** and **Human Dimension** aims that cognitive taxonomies miss. Discipleship pathways are scaffolding-and-fading made concrete: high structure for new believers (Grow's Stage 1), faded toward self-directed practice and peer leadership. Bloom/SOLO help write small-group questions that climb from recall to relational reflection.

### Creative / Content
Teaching a craft benefits most from the **whole-task/problem-centered** family. 4C/ID and Merrill argue against the common tutorial mistake of front-loading isolated theory: start with an authentic whole task, supply supportive info as needed, give procedural info just-in-time, reserve drills (scales, typography exercises, code katas) for routines that must become automatic. Reigeluth's zoom lens is excellent tutorial-series architecture (epitome project → components → re-synthesize). Action mapping translates cleanly to creator goals ("learners will publish a finished X"), keeping tutorials practice-first and pushing reference detail into downloadable cheat sheets. Design spaced retrieval in from the start.

## Quick-Reference Heuristics

- **Could they do it if their life depended on it?** If yes, it's not a training problem — fix the environment.
- **Default to a job aid** for look-up-able, infrequent, or frequently-changing tasks (especially software/EHR).
- **Backward design:** measurable objective → evidence/assessment → activities. Map objectives on the Anderson–Krathwohl table so you're not stuck at "Remember."
- **Whole-task first** for complex skills (4C/ID); drill only to automate routines.
- **Gagné's nine events** as the lesson skeleton; **Merrill's First Principles** as the quality checklist.
- **Match the process model to the project:** SAM/Agile for fast iterative digital builds; Dick & Carey/ADDIE for large sign-off-driven programs.
- **Engineer spaced retrieval and transfer tasks in from the start** (Phase 06).
