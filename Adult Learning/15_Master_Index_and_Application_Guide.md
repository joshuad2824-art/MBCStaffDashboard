When you need the evidence behind a claim, go to the named phase. Drop this file (and `00_Research_Library_Master_Plan.md`) into every project so each one knows what's available.

## What this library is, in one paragraph

Sixteen documents (a master plan, fourteen subject phases, two built syntheses) covering how adults learn, how to design instruction, how to carry ideas to an audience, and how to design the artifacts that hold it all. It was built once to be shared across every project so each can pull what's relevant. The defining commitment throughout is **honest evidence grading**: every phase separates what's well-supported from what's craft wisdom from what's myth, so you can lean confidently on the strong findings and hold the weak ones lightly.

## Recommended reading orders

Pick the path that matches the job in front of you.

**If you have time for only three phases (the highest-leverage core):** 05 (Cognitive Load) → 06 (Memory) → 07 (Instructional Design). Together they upgrade nearly every training artifact you build.

**"I'm building a training / onboarding kit":** 07 → 05 → 06 → 08 → 11 → 13. (Design backward, manage load, build in retrieval, plan motivation and evaluation, then make the artifacts clean and accessible.)

**"I'm making a quick-reference tip sheet or job aid":** 13 → 11 → 05 → 07 (the train-vs-job-aid decision). Short and decisive.

**"I'm preparing a talk, lesson, in-service, or sermon":** 09 (structure the narrative) → 10 (design and deliver it) → 14 (if it's discussion-based). Add 06 if retention matters.

**"I'm leading a small group / running a discussion / facilitating a workshop":** 14 → 01–04 (who the learners are) → 02 (reflection) → 08 (motivation, feedback). Wait-time and psychological safety from 14 are the immediate wins.

**"I'm designing a flyer, slide deck, bulletin, report, or brand":** 11 → 12 → 13. (Grammar, then data/information design, then accessibility.)

**"I want the whole theory foundation":** read Part I in order (01 → 02 → 03 → 04), then Part II (05 → 06). Everything else builds on these.

**"Just give me the argument of the whole library":** read 04 (the adult-learning synthesis) and this file. The through-lines below carry the rest.

## The through-lines (what the whole library actually argues)

Five ideas recur across every phase and unify the library. If you internalize only these, you have most of the value.

1. **Structure first, fade to autonomy.** The single most cross-validated claim. Andragogy, experiential learning, situated learning, cognitive load, instructional design, and facilitation all arrive at it independently: novices need guidance they can't yet supply themselves; autonomy is the *destination*, staged toward, not the starting method. Diagnose stage (not age) per domain, scaffold heavily, then fade. *(01–07, 14.)*

2. **The skilled facilitator/teacher is the highest-leverage investment.** Across experiential learning, communities of practice, action learning, and simulation, the recurring finding is that the *person guiding the process* — not the activity, the technology, or the content — determines whether learning happens. *(02, 03, 08, 14.)*

3. **Ease in the moment is not the same as durable learning.** Reduce *extraneous* difficulty (bad design, clutter, redundancy — Phase 5) but introduce *desirable* difficulty (retrieval, spacing, interleaving — Phase 6). Fluency feels like learning and usually isn't. *(05, 06.)*

4. **The curse of knowledge is the root of most bad communication.** Experts forget what it's like not to know, so they skip the concrete, sequential scaffolding novices need. It's the same phenomenon as the expertise-reversal effect and the expert blind spot. Story, plain language, teach-back, and reader testing are the antidotes — they force you to verify understanding from the audience's side. *(05, 09, 13.)*

5. **Clarity and honesty are ethical floors, not stylistic choices.** A chart can mislead as powerfully as a story; obscurity excludes and (in health) harms; persuasion that bypasses understanding is manipulation. The library holds a consistent line: honest communication serves the audience's understanding and agency. *(09, 12, 13.)*

A sixth, meta-level commitment runs underneath all of it: **grade the evidence honestly.** The library repeatedly separates the strong (cognitive load, retrieval/spacing, graphical perception, wait-time, psychological safety, plain language) from the contested (transfer of retrieval practice to complex tasks, the data-ink/chartjunk debate) from the popular-but-weak (learning styles, the golden ratio, universal color psychology, power posing, Mehrabian's 7-38-55, Tuckman's strict stages, groupthink's causal model, UDL's outcome evidence). Lean on the first set; hold the rest lightly.

## The cross-link web

Each phase points to its neighbors. The dense connections:

- **01–04 → 05–06.** Part I keeps arriving at the novice/structure problem; Part II is the *mechanism* that explains it (working memory, expertise reversal, durable-learning techniques).
- **05–06 → 07.** Cognitive science becomes buildable design: chunking, sequencing, worked-example fading, and spaced retrieval engineered into the artifact.
- **07 ↔ 08.** Design and evaluation bracket the instructional-design Part: decide what learners must *do*, then measure whether they can do it; the assumed chain from satisfaction to results is weak, so design backward from performance.
- **09 → 10.** Narrative/rhetoric structure becomes concrete presentation craft; the assertion-evidence model is literally Phase 5's multimedia principles applied to slides.
- **11 → 12 → 13.** Visual grammar → information/data design → reaching everyone. Dataviz is applied graphical perception; accessibility is the floor beneath all of it.
- **14 ← 02, 03, 08.** Facilitation delivers the "skilled facilitator" the earlier phases kept demanding, and supplies the psychological safety that honest reflection, transformative learning, and productive feedback all require.
- **Ethics spine: 09 ↔ 12 ↔ 13.** Persuasion-vs-manipulation (story), honesty-in-charts (data), and clarity-as-duty (accessibility) are one ethic in three settings.

## How to query the library from inside a project

When you're in a project and want the library to inform the work, a few patterns that work well:

- **Name the artifact and the audience, and ask which phases apply.** "I'm writing a discharge instruction for low-literacy patients — what does the library say?" routes to 13 → 11 → 12 → 05.
- **Ask for the *strong* findings only when you need confidence.** "What's the best-evidenced way to make this training stick?" → 06 (retrieval, spacing) and 05 (cognitive load), the high-confidence core.
- **Ask for the *decision*, not the survey, when you're choosing.** "Should this be a course or a job aid?" → the train-vs-support decision in 07. "Bar chart or pie?" → graphical-perception hierarchy in 12.
- **Invoke the through-lines as shorthand.** "Apply 'structure first, fade to autonomy' to this onboarding path" or "run this past the curse-of-knowledge check" gets you straight to the operative principle.
- **When two phases conflict, the more specific and better-evidenced wins.** The library flags conflicts explicitly (e.g., reduce extraneous difficulty *and* add desirable difficulty); the resolution is almost always "match it to the learner's expertise and the task."

## Note on format

All sixteen files now follow one uniform schema, so they browse cleanly side by side:

**Header** (Part · Applies-to tags · Type · Status) → **Bottom Line** → **Core Principles** → **Key Thinkers & Seminal Sources** → **Deep Dive** → **Evidence Base & Open Debates** → **Application by Context** (Clinical/Training · Ministry/Teaching · Creative/Content) → **Quick-Reference Heuristics** → **When to Reach for This** → **Connections to Other Phases** → **Sources & Further Reading**, closing with a short italic evidence note.

The thirteen research phases were originally delivered in a deep-research report format (TL;DR / Key Findings / Details / Recommendations / Caveats) and have since been reformatted into the schema above, with every effect size, citation, and evidence grade preserved verbatim. The applies-to tags and the explicit "Connections to Other Phases" sections now live in each file's own header rather than only here in the index. Phases 04 and 15 are the two built (not researched) syntheses and have followed the schema from the start; they're the two to reach for first when you want the argument without the full evidence trail.
