# Mark & Measure — A Design Practice Research Library
## Master Plan & Build Spec
### Version 1.0 | June 2026

*A reusable reference library on logo design, typography, and the craft and business of graphic design. Built once, drawn from for Joshua's own work, for helping friends and small businesses, and — should the road lead there — for a design practice of his own.*

---

## 0. How to Use This Document

This is the **spec**, not the research itself. It defines what we're building, the order we'll build it in, the shape every output file will take, and — for each phase — exactly what the research run needs to chase down. Keep it at the front of the library; it tells you what exists, what each piece is for, and where to reach when a real project lands on the desk.

The library is built in **phases**. Most phases are a single deep-research run: the research goes out across the authoritative sources, the findings come back, and I reformat them into a schema-compliant markdown file — evidence graded, cross-linked, tagged. Two pieces I build by hand. You can stop after any *Part* and still have something whole and usable.

**This is a sibling library, not a replacement.** Your parent **Learning, Communication & Design Research Library** already lays the *grammar* of visual design in its **Phase 11** — type anatomy, classification, Gestalt, color theory, C.R.A.P., grids, the honest myth-grading. *Mark & Measure* does not re-prove that grammar. It goes **deeper** on the three crafts Phase 11 only touches, and adds the **practitioner and business layers** Phase 11 never reaches. Where the two meet, this library **docks to Phase 11** and builds upward.

## 1. The Shared Output Schema

Every researched phase produces one markdown file with these sections, in this order. Consistency is what makes a stack of files into a library — you can jump to "Application by Context" in any file and know exactly what you're looking at.

```
# [Phase N] — Title
Part: ...
Applies to: [Own Work] [Friends & Small Business] [Studio / Client] [Universal]
Status: Pending / Researched [date]

## Bottom Line
One paragraph. The durable core a busy project can read and act on.

## Core Principles
The load-bearing, citable claims. Each gets a one-line "why it matters."

## Key Thinkers & Seminal Sources
Person — their contribution — the work to actually read or look at.

## Deep Dive
The substantive body, organized by sub-topic. This is the researched content.

## Evidence Base & Open Debates
What's well-supported, what's craft consensus, what's outright myth.
Honesty here is the point — we grade design lore, we don't launder it as law.

## Application by Context
### Own Work
### Friends & Small Business
### Studio / Client
Concrete moves, not platitudes.

## Quick-Reference Heuristics
Checklist / rules of thumb for fast recall.

## When to Reach for This
Decision guidance — the conditions under which this material is the right tool.

## Connections to Other Phases
Cross-links, within this library and to the parent library. This is the connective tissue.

## Sources & Further Reading
```

## 2. Tagging Convention

Each file (and where useful, each sub-section) carries one or more **Applies-to** tags, keyed to the three jobs this library serves:

- **[Own Work]** — Joshua's own brands and projects: Timber & Ink, and whatever comes next.
- **[Friends & Small Business]** — helping people he knows make something good without the full studio apparatus.
- **[Studio / Client]** — professional, paid, contracted work; the side-business path.
- **[Universal]** — foundational craft that serves all three.

Most *craft* phases are [Universal] — a good logo is a good logo whoever it's for. The *business* and *people* phases are where the audience tags do real filtering work.

## 3. The Evidence Ethic

This library inherits the parent's spine: **honest grading.** Design carries a great deal of repeated lore — some of it durable craft wisdom, some of it marketing overreach, some of it outright myth dressed as law. Every phase grades its major claims into three bands:

- **Strong** — supported by real evidence (perceptual science, controlled study).
- **Craft consensus** — the well-tested professional judgment of the field's best, valuable but not lab-proven. *Most of graphic design lives here, and that's fine — we just name it honestly.*
- **Myth / overstated** — repeated as gospel, weak or false on inspection (the golden ratio as a beauty law, the construction-grid overlay as proof of rigor, universal "color psychology").

This isn't academic fussiness. It's what keeps the library from quietly teaching you folk wisdom you'd then pass to a paying client.

## 4. Workflow — How We Run Each Phase

1. **You** tell me which phase to build (default: in order).
2. **I** run the deep research for that phase, scoped tightly to the brief below so it goes deep instead of wandering. *(Each phase brief doubles as a ready-to-run research prompt — so a phase can also be run in a fresh chat if you'd rather.)*
3. The findings come back.
4. **I** convert them into a schema-compliant `.md` file — tags, evidence grades, cross-links, the Application-by-Context sections — and hand it back for review.
5. **You** review; we adjust; it ships.
6. After a Part is complete, I can build any synthesis that draws on it.

Built in passes, built in storms — not on a calendar. Each phase is a standalone artifact, so there's no rigid slog. We move when the next storm hits.

### PART I — TYPE & LETTERFORM

#### Phase 01 — Typography in Depth
**Applies to:** [Universal]
**Scope:** Typography at practitioner depth — past the Phase-11 grammar. Selecting, pairing, and *setting* type beautifully and legibly across media; the modern type technology; expressive versus functional type.
**Chase down:**
- Deep classification (Vox-ATypI and its limits) and what each historical family *signals* — so classification informs choice, not just naming.
- The craft of **setting** type: measure and leading by size, tracking discipline, ligatures, true small caps, old-style vs. lining figures, widows/orphans, rag quality, justification, hanging punctuation, optical margin alignment.
- **Type pairing as a system**, not a vibe: superfamilies, contrast vs. harmony strategies, x-height matching, the common pairing failures.
- Building a **type scale / hierarchy** (modular scales, type ramps) and applying it consistently.
- **Display & expressive** type — type that carries personality; when to break the rules.
- The **technology**: OpenType features, variable fonts (axes and why they matter), web-font delivery and performance (FOUT/FOIT), and **font licensing** (desktop vs. web vs. app — a real landmine for client work).
- Screen vs. print typography; rendering, hinting, responsive type.
- Type **accessibility** — minimum sizes, line length, and an honest grade on "dyslexia-friendly" fonts.
**Must surface:** Robert Bringhurst (*The Elements of Typographic Style*); Ellen Lupton (*Thinking with Type*); Matthew Butterick (*Practical Typography*); Erik Spiekermann (*Stop Stealing Sheep*); Cyrus Highsmith (*Inside Paragraphs*); Tim Brown (*Flexible Typesetting*, modular scale); the OpenType/variable-font specs; the honest evidence on OpenDyslexic and similar.
**Dock:** Carries Phase 11's parallel-letter-recognition model and serif-vs-sans verdict — does not re-litigate them.

#### Phase 02 — Lettering, Custom Type & Wordmarks
**Applies to:** [Universal]
**Scope:** The hand and the custom mark — lettering, calligraphy, and *drawing* letterforms for wordmarks and logotypes. The bridge from typography into logo design.
**Chase down:**
- Lettering vs. calligraphy vs. type design — the three disciplines and what each is for.
- Drawing letters: skeletons, strokes, contrast, terminals, and **optical correction** (overshoot, the corrections that make straights, curves, and diagonals *look* even when they aren't).
- Building a **custom wordmark/logotype** from or against an existing typeface — modifying letterforms, custom ligatures, spacing a logotype (the rhythm of black and white).
- Script, brush, and monoline lettering; the role of grids and guides.
- The **digitization** workflow — sketch to vector.
- When custom is worth it vs. licensing a typeface (the cost/benefit for a real client).
**Must surface:** Jessica Hische (*In Progress*; the lettering process); Doyald Young (*Logotypes & Letterforms*); Martina Flor (*The Golden Secrets of Lettering*); Ken Barber / House Industries; Tobias Frere-Jones on optical correction and spacing.
**Dock:** Feeds Phase 04 (logotype construction) directly.

### PART II — LOGO & MARK DESIGN

#### Phase 03 — Logo Principles & Anatomy
**Applies to:** [Universal]
**Scope:** What makes a logo *work* — the principles, the taxonomy of logo types, and the judgment to tell a strong mark from a weak one.
**Chase down:**
- The classic principles (simple, memorable, timeless, versatile, appropriate) — and an **honest grade**: which are durable truth, which are repeated slogan.
- The **taxonomy** of logo types — wordmark/logotype, lettermark/monogram, pictorial symbol, abstract mark, mascot, combination, emblem — strengths, weaknesses, fit.
- Logo vs. brand vs. identity — what a logo can and cannot do (sets up Phase 05).
- Anatomy of strong marks through **case studies** (FedEx, NBC, Rand's IBM/UPS/NeXT, the WWF panda, Glaser's I♥NY, the London Underground roundel) — negative space, dual meaning, the silhouette/black-and-white test, the "describe it over the phone" test.
- **Trends vs. timelessness** — the fad cycle (gradients, "blanding," sans-serif sameness) and how to resist designing for the moment.
- Clichés and crimes — the swoosh, the globe, the lightbulb, the unscalable over-detailed mark.
**Must surface:** Paul Rand (*A Designer's Art*; "Logos… Escutcheons"); David Airey (*Logo Design Love*); Sagi Haviv & Chermayeff & Geismar (*Identify* — appropriate, distinctive, simple, memorable); Saul Bass; Michael Bierut (*How to*); Aaron Draplin; Milton Glaser.
**Dock:** Grades the principles as craft consensus, not lab science (the Evidence Ethic).

#### Phase 04 — The Logo Design Process
**Applies to:** [Universal] [Studio / Client]
**Scope:** The repeatable workflow from blank page to delivered mark — followable by a careful non-designer, relied on by pros.
**Chase down:**
- **Discovery & the brief** — the questions to ask; understanding the business, audience, and competitors; the brief as contract-of-intent.
- **Research** — competitive audit and visual research that positions the mark to stand apart.
- **Divergence** — thumbnailing, quantity over quality, why you sketch before you vector, the ideation drills.
- **Convergence & construction** — refining the direction; construction grids and geometry; **optical correction** — and an honest line on when geometry helps versus when "golden ratio" overlays are retrofitted theater.
- Logotype selection/customization (docks to Phase 02).
- **Color & the logo suite** — build in one color first; primary/secondary/mark-only, horizontal/stacked, reversed, monochrome.
- **Testing** — favicon-to-billboard scalability, black-and-white, the squint test.
- **Presentation & rationale** — present one strong direction with a story, not options-soup; mockups in context (docks to Phase 08).
- **Delivery** — what to hand off (docks to Phase 10).
**Must surface:** David Airey (process; presenting a single direction); Sagi Haviv (process & rationale); Von Glitschka (*Vector Basic Training* — thumbnail, reference, sketch, vector); Aaron Draplin (the live logo demos).
**Dock:** Carries Phase 11's golden-ratio skepticism into the construction-grid question.

#### Phase 05 — Identity & Brand Systems
**Applies to:** [Universal] [Friends & Small Business] [Studio / Client]
**Scope:** Beyond the logo — building and documenting a full visual identity, and the brand-strategy fundamentals that should drive it.
**Chase down:**
- Clean definitions: brand vs. brand identity vs. logo vs. brand strategy — what each layer does.
- **Brand strategy a designer actually needs** — positioning, audience, brand attributes/personality, the promise — enough to make design decisions defensible (not a full marketing course).
- The **components of a visual identity system**: logo suite, applied color system, type system, photography/imagery style, iconography, graphic devices/motifs, layout systems, voice (light touch).
- **Brand guidelines** — what belongs in a usable guide, scaled from a one-pager for a friend to a full system for a client; real examples.
- Consistency vs. flexibility; design tokens (docks to Phase 11 / Atomic Design).
- **Identity in application** — stationery, social, signage, packaging, web; the "does it hold across touchpoints" test.
- **Rebrands** — evolve vs. revolution; the "blanding" risk.
**Must surface:** Alina Wheeler (*Designing Brand Identity* — the standard reference); Marty Neumeier (*The Brand Gap*, *Zag*); Wally Olins (*The Brand Handbook*); Chermayeff & Geismar; Pentagram case studies.
**Dock:** Inherits Phase 11's honest color-psychology caveat; reuses its design-token vocabulary.

### PART III — GRAPHIC DESIGN CRAFT

#### Phase 06 — Composition & Layout in Practice
**Applies to:** [Universal]
**Scope:** Putting elements on a page with intention — applied layout past the C.R.A.P. grammar, for real artifacts: posters, flyers, social, editorial, packaging.
**Chase down:**
- **Advanced grids in practice** — column, modular, baseline; breaking the grid on purpose; the grid as freedom, not cage.
- **Composition & the eye** — focal points, visual flow, tension, balance (symmetry vs. dynamic asymmetry), scale and contrast as drama, the active use of white space.
- **Type + image integration** — text over image, captions, pull quotes, word-and-picture relationships.
- **Format-specific craft** — editorial/page layout, the poster (one big idea), social graphics and carousels, packaging basics.
- **Visual sequence** — pacing across a multi-page or multi-slide piece.
**Must surface:** Josef Müller-Brockmann (*Grid Systems*); Ellen Lupton & Jennifer Cole Phillips (*Graphic Design: The New Basics*); Timothy Samara (*Making and Breaking the Grid*); Massimo Vignelli (*The Vignelli Canon* — discipline and restraint); Jan Tschichold.
**Dock:** Carries Phase 11's skepticism on golden ratio / rule of thirds as defaults, not laws.

#### Phase 07 — Design History & Movements
**Applies to:** [Universal]
**Scope:** The literacy that builds taste and judgment — the movements, eras, and designers whose work is the field's shared vocabulary.
**Chase down:**
- The arc: Arts & Crafts → Art Nouveau → early modernism → Bauhaus → the Swiss/International Typographic Style → mid-century American (the golden age of the logo) → postmodern/New Wave → digital → now.
- The movements that matter most to a working designer — Bauhaus, Swiss/International, and the reactions against them.
- The designers worth knowing **and why** — Rand, Bass, Vignelli, Müller-Brockmann, Lubalin, Scher, Glaser, Carson, Sagmeister, Chermayeff & Geismar.
- How historical literacy improves practice — borrowing intelligently, avoiding pastiche, recognizing the recycled "fresh" idea.
- A clear-eyed note on the canon's blind spots — whose work the standard histories under-credit.
**Must surface:** Philip B. Meggs (*Meggs' History of Graphic Design* — the standard text); Steven Heller; *The Vignelli Canon*; *Helvetica* (Hustwit) as an accessible entry; the core movements (Bauhaus, De Stijl, Constructivism, Swiss Style).
**Frame:** Taste-building, not trivia.

### PART IV — THE PRACTICE & THE BUSINESS

#### Phase 08 — Critique, Feedback & Presenting Work
**Applies to:** [Universal] [Friends & Small Business] [Studio / Client]
**Scope:** The human side — judging your own work, giving and taking critique, and presenting/defending design so it survives contact with a client or a friend.
**Chase down:**
- **Critique as a skill** — running and receiving it; separating "I don't like it" from "it doesn't work"; frameworks like "I like / I wish / I wonder."
- **Self-critique & getting unstuck** — distance, the squint test, sleeping on it, killing darlings.
- **Presenting work** — the rationale presentation (sell the thinking, not the pixels); one strong direction vs. a buffet; mockups and context.
- **Handling feedback & revisions** — translating "make it pop" into a design decision; feedback that improves vs. erodes; scope creep and the endless-revision trap.
- **The hard conversations** — pushing back respectfully, the friend-as-client dynamic, saying no, protecting both the work and the relationship.
**Must surface:** Mike Monteiro (*Design Is a Job*, *You're My Favorite Client*); Michael Bierut (*How to*); Adam Connor & Aaron Irizarry (*Discussing Design*); the Stanford d.school "I like / I wish / I wonder" frame.
**Dock:** Pulls informational-vs-controlling feedback (parent Phase 08 / SDT) and psychological safety (parent Phase 14).

#### Phase 09 — The Design Business
**Applies to:** [Studio / Client] [Friends & Small Business]
**Scope:** The seed of a side practice — pricing, contracts, process, deliverables, and the ethics of doing design for money (and for friends).
**Chase down:**
- **Pricing** — hourly vs. project vs. value-based; how to estimate; what logo/identity work costs at different tiers; the friends-and-family pricing trap and how to do discounted/pro-bono work without resentment.
- **The paperwork** — proposals, the brief-as-agreement, contracts (scope, deliverables, revision limits, kill fee, IP/usage transfer, payment terms) and why a contract protects the relationship.
- **Client management** — onboarding, deposits, milestones, communication cadence, the revision-round structure.
- **Deliverables & handoff** — what a client actually receives (formats — docks to Phase 10 — plus a brand guide); and the **font/asset licensing** landmine.
- **Positioning a small practice** — portfolio, niche, first clients, word-of-mouth; the ethics of spec work (No!Spec); honest AI-and-design disclosure.
- **Doing it with integrity** — honest pricing, stewardship of relationships, when to say no. *Light touch, in Joshua's frame — faith as root, not billboard.*
**Must surface:** Mike Monteiro (*Design Is a Job*); David Airey (*Work for Money, Design for Love*); the AIGA Standards of Professional Practice; the No!Spec position; font-licensing/EULA realities; value-based pricing discourse (Blair Enns, *Pricing Creativity* — light).

#### Phase 10 — Tools, Files & Production
**Applies to:** [Universal] [Studio / Client]
**Scope:** The technical floor — software, file formats, and print/digital production knowledge that turns a design into a correct, usable deliverable.
**Chase down:**
- **Vector vs. raster** — the core distinction, why logos must be vector, resolution / DPI / PPI.
- **The tools** — Illustrator, Affinity Designer, Inkscape (free), Figma, and where Canva fits and stops for the real workflow; Photoshop for raster.
- **File formats decoded** — SVG, EPS, AI, PDF (print vs. interactive), PNG, JPG, WebP — which to deliver for which use.
- **Color for production** — RGB vs. CMYK vs. spot/Pantone; profiles; why screen ≠ print; the recent Pantone licensing changes (**verify — this dates fast**).
- **Print production** — bleed, trim, safe margins, resolution, paper basics, proofs, working with a printer.
- **Digital/export** — sizes for web and social, favicons, responsive assets, the logo export "kit."
- **The deliverable package** — organizing a handoff a non-designer can actually use.
**Must surface:** current Adobe / Affinity / Figma documentation (verify versions in research); print-production standards; SVG/web best practices; Von Glitschka (vector technique).
**Flag:** The phase most prone to staleness — date-stamp every tool/version/pricing specific.
