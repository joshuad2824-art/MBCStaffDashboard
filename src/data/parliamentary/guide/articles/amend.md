# Amending a motion

**Short answer:** An amendment changes the wording of a motion before the group votes on it. It takes a majority, and it has to relate to the motion it's changing. You can amend an amendment, but that's the limit: two levels at a time.

`Second: Yes · Debate: Yes (unless the motion it changes can't be debated) · Amend: Yes, once · Vote: Majority · Interrupt a speaker: No`

**What to say:** Name the change and where it goes:

- "I move to amend by inserting 'and a printer' after 'copier.'"
- "I move to amend by striking out '$2,500.'"
- "I move to amend by striking out '$2,500' and inserting '$3,000.'"
- "I move to substitute the following for the motion: …"

## How it works

**There are three kinds of amendment.** You can **insert or add** words, **strike out** words, or **strike out and insert**, which replaces some words with others. Replacing a whole paragraph or the entire motion is called a **substitute**.

**One at a time, two levels deep.** Only one amendment to the motion can be pending at a time, plus one amendment to that amendment. A third level isn't allowed. If you've got another idea, say you'll offer it if the current one fails. Any number of amendments can be offered one after another.

**The vote order runs backward.** The group votes on the amendment to the amendment first, then on the amendment, then on the motion as it now stands. After each vote, the chair should read what the motion now says: "The question is now on the motion as amended, which reads…"

**Passing an amendment doesn't pass the motion.** That's true even of a substitute that replaced the entire motion. Once the substitute is adopted, the group still has to vote on the motion itself.

**It has to relate to the motion.** The amendment has to be "germane," meaning on the same subject. It can even run against what the mover intended. Changing "censure" to "thanks" is in order, because both are about how the group views someone's conduct. But adding "and pay the delegates' travel" to a motion to buy a desk isn't, because it's a different subject entirely.

**Amendments that aren't in order:**

- adding "not" so that voting yes on the amendment means the same as voting no on the motion
- repeating something the group already decided this session
- turning the motion into a different kind of motion
- leaving the motion making no sense
- anything frivolous

To change "adopt" to "reject," move to [postpone indefinitely](postpone-indefinitely.md) instead.

**Once words are in or out, that's settled.** Words the group voted to insert can't then be struck out, and words it voted to strike can't be put back in the same place. The only exception is a change big enough to make it a new question. Otherwise, the way back is to [reconsider](reconsider.md).

**Many changes at once?** Write out a clean version and offer it as a substitute. If it needs real work, [refer it to a committee](refer-to-committee.md).

## Filling in a blank

When the question is a name, a number, a date, or an amount, the group can leave a blank and let members suggest options. No second is needed. Each person suggests one option, and the group votes on them one at a time in order, stopping at the first one that gets a majority.

For money, Robert's says to start with whichever option is least likely to pass. When the group is deciding how much to *spend*, vote on the largest amount first and work down. When it's deciding the price to *sell* something for, start with the smallest and work up. That way the group lands on the figure most people can actually agree on.

## Watch out for

- **"Friendly amendments."** The person who made the motion can say, "I accept the amendment," but that only works if no one objects. If anyone does, the amendment gets a regular vote.
- **Amending something already adopted.** That isn't this motion. It's a new main motion, with its own rules (see [Rescind](rescind.md)).
- **The chair ruling an amendment out of order.** Robert's says the chair should only do that when it's clearly out of order. When in doubt, let the group decide.
- **Fixing the minutes.** Corrections are usually made informally when someone points them out. After the minutes are approved, a change takes two-thirds, or a majority with advance notice.

## At MBC

- **Budget.** At the special business meeting where the church votes on the budget, no amendments are allowed. It's an "approve" or "oppose" vote, decided by simple majority (Policy A009 ¶2.3). Changes have to happen earlier in the process.
- **Bylaws.** Bylaw amendments have their own notice requirements (Bylaws Art. V). See [Amending the bylaws](amending-the-bylaws.md).
