# Amending the bylaws

**Short answer:** Bylaws are changed by whatever process the bylaws themselves set. Robert's standard is advance notice plus a two-thirds vote. MBC's bylaws set their own process: a written proposal discussed at a business meeting at least a month ahead, public notice each Sunday after that, and then a simple majority of those present and voting.

## Robert's rules for amending

**If the bylaws are silent** on how to amend them, Robert's default applies. An amendment then takes either:

- a majority of the entire membership at any regular business meeting, or
- two-thirds of those voting, with a quorum present, if the amendment was submitted in writing at the previous regular business meeting.

**Robert's advice for writing an amendment clause:**

- Always require notice. Robert's recommends "the previous regular meeting," not "a previous" one, so the notice can't be stretched indefinitely.
- Avoid "two-thirds of the members," which in most groups is almost never present.
- One word, "amend," covers any change, including a complete replacement. "Amend, alter, or add to" just repeats itself.

**Amending the amendment.** While a bylaw amendment is pending, it can be changed by majority vote without new notice. But only within the scope of the notice. If the notice said dues would go from $2 to $5, the group can settle anywhere from $2 to $5, but not $6 or $1. Members who didn't come because they could live with $5 deserve that protection. The same goes for a substitute: it can scale back the changes, but it can't add new ones.

**Notice.** Unless the rules require written notice, notice can be oral, and only the gist of the change is needed, not the exact text. That's unless the rule requires the amendment itself to be submitted. If a committee was appointed to revise the bylaws and report at a set meeting, that appointment can count as the notice, if the bylaws only require previous notice.

**Taking effect.** An amendment takes effect the moment it's adopted unless the motion says otherwise. To delay it, add a proviso like "This takes effect October 1," or adopt a separate motion by majority before the vote.

**Officers already elected.** An amendment can change an existing officer's duties, or even abolish the office. If that isn't intended, say so in the motion.

**Reconsidering.** A yes vote on a bylaw amendment can't be reconsidered (see [Reconsider](reconsider.md)). A no vote can.

## Watch out for

- **Skipping the notice.** The notice protects members who aren't in the room. It can't be waived, even unanimously (see [Votes that don't count](votes-that-dont-count.md)).
- **Floor amendments that go beyond the notice.** These are out of order, however reasonable they sound.
- **Going paragraph by paragraph.** A full revision should be considered paragraph by paragraph, with one vote at the end (see [Divide the question](divide-the-question.md)).

## At MBC

**Bylaws (Art. V).** Our bylaws can be amended:

- at any business meeting, or at one specially called for the purpose
- with the proposed amendment included in the call for the meeting
- after the change has been laid before the church in writing and discussed at a business meeting at least one month before the vote
- after it's been read from the pulpit each Sunday following, or published weekly in a regular church publication
- by a **simple majority of members present and voting**

Robert's two-thirds default doesn't apply. Robert's says that default is "only in force when the by-laws are silent," and ours aren't.

**Constitution.** Art. V speaks only of "these bylaws," and the constitution in our corpus has no amendment clause of its own. If that's right, Robert's default for documents that are silent would apply to the constitution, including its adoption of the Baptist Faith & Message 2000 as the Articles of Faith. Under that default, an amendment takes either a majority of the entire membership, or two-thirds of those voting after written submission at the previous regular business meeting. This is a reading of the corpus as transcribed. It should be confirmed before anyone relies on it.
