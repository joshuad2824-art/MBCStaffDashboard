# Committees and boards — how they work

**Short answer:** A committee is a smaller group that does work for the larger body. A *standing* committee serves for a set term. A *special* committee exists for one task and ends when it reports. Committees run with less formality than the full body: no seconds, no limit on speaking, and the chair takes part. But every decision is still made by vote, and a majority of the members is a quorum.

## Kinds of committees

- **Boards and executive committees.** These are small decision-making bodies that act for a larger organization between its meetings, with whatever authority the parent body gives them. A board can't overturn an action of the body above it.
- **Standing committees.** Appointed for a set term, like a year, to handle a continuing area of work. A standing committee has to report on everything referred to it by the end of its term.
- **Special committees.** Appointed for one task. They last until the task is done, or until the parent body discharges them. Once the parent body receives the final report, the committee no longer exists.

## How a committee meets

- **The chair calls the meetings.** If the chair won't or can't, any two members can call one, as long as every member is notified.
- **A majority of the members is a quorum,** unless the parent body sets something different.
- **It runs informally,** with no seconds, no limit on how many times someone speaks, and no need to stand. Motions to close or limit discussion aren't allowed. The chair is usually the most active participant, and doesn't step aside to speak or make motions. Every question still goes to a vote unless there's general consent.
- **Members of the parent body** can ask to appear and give their views at a time the committee sets. During the committee's own deliberations, only committee members are present.
- **Reconsidering is easier.** A vote can be reconsidered at any time, by anyone who didn't vote on the losing side. It takes two-thirds, unless everyone who voted on the winning side is present or was notified ahead of time.
- **What happens stays in the committee.** Nobody refers in the full body to what was said in committee, except through the committee's report or by general consent.
- **A committee can't discipline its members.** It reports the facts to the parent body.
- **Subcommittees** report to the committee, never directly to the parent body.

## Who chairs

- **Whoever appoints the committee** can name its chairman.
- **If no chairman is named,** the first person named serves until the committee elects its own. A standing committee usually elects its own.
- **Committees usually elect a secretary** to keep a brief record of what's done.

## Ex officio members

An ex officio member is on a board or committee because of another office they hold. When they leave that office, they leave the committee too.

- **If they're a member of the organization,** they're a full member with all the same rights and duties.
- **If the president is ex officio on every committee,** Robert's reads that as *permitting* them to take part, not *requiring* it. So they aren't counted when figuring the quorum.
- **If they're not under the organization's authority,** they have every privilege of membership, including the vote, but none of the obligations.

## Who should serve

- **A committee to carry out a decision** should be small and made up of people who support it.
- **A committee to investigate or deliberate** should be larger and represent every viewpoint, so its findings carry weight. Robert's says: "When a committee is properly selected, in nine cases out of ten its action decides that of the assembly."

## At MBC

**The Deacon Board's standing committees.** The bylaws set them up and say the Deacon Chair appoints their chairpersons, in consultation with the Senior Pastor (Bylaws Art. II.B §3). The bylaws also set some committees' sizes. Finance has seven members, and Personnel has five.

**The church's standing committees.** These are listed in Art. III §3, and each has a chairperson and at least two members. Their chairpersons and members are selected each year by the Nominating Committee and elected by the church, and they serve October 1 through September 30 (Art. III §3). Where the bylaws name the chair or set the size, Robert's defaults on those points don't apply.

**Ex officio members.** The bylaws name one ex officio seat: a pastoral staff member on the Missions Committee (Art. III §3 ¶7).
