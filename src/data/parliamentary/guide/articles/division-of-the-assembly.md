# Asking for a counted vote — "Division of the Assembly"

**Short answer:** If a voice vote or show of hands isn't clear, any one member can call out "Division!" and the chair has to retake the vote standing. It needs no second and no vote. Getting an actual number counted, a ballot, or a roll call takes a motion and a majority.

`Second: No · Debate: No · Amend: No · Vote: None — any member can demand it · Interrupt a speaker: Yes`

**What to say:** "Division!" or "I call for a division," or "I doubt the vote." You don't need the floor.

## How it works

**It can come late.** You can call for it any time after the question's been put, even after the result's been announced and someone else has the floor. The only conditions are that the vote was by voice or show of hands and that no other motion has been made yet.

**The chair retakes the vote standing.** "Those in favor, please stand. Be seated. Those opposed, please stand." Discussion can't start back up during the second vote unless no one objects.

**A count is a separate step.** A standing vote lets everyone see the result, but it isn't necessarily counted. To have it counted, or to switch to a ballot or a roll call, someone moves it, and the group decides by majority without discussion. Usually the chair just counts if it's close. When several methods are suggested, the group votes first on the one that takes the most time.

**The chair can do this unasked.** If the chair isn't sure of a voice vote, they can retake it standing, and even count it, without anyone calling for a division.

## Watch out for

- **Calling for division when the result is obvious.** Robert's says the chair shouldn't let members use it just to annoy the meeting when the vote was full and clear. Repeated calls like that can be treated as stalling (see [Stalling and nuisance motions](dilatory-motions.md)).
- **Waiting until the next motion.** Once another motion's been made, it's too late.
