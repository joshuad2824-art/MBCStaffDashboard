# Getting the floor

**Short answer:** You can't make a motion or speak to one until the chair recognizes you. Get the chair's attention, address them by title, and wait to be recognized. Once you have the floor, nobody can interrupt you except for a short list of urgent reasons.

**What to say:** "Mr. Chairman." In a small group where everyone knows each other, the chair can just nod or say your name.

## How it works

**First one up after the floor's open gets it, as a rule.** If two people go for the floor at about the same time, the one who got the chair's attention first after the last speaker finished usually gets it. You can't claim you were first if you were already standing or waving before the last speaker was done.

**But the chair should sometimes recognize someone else first.** Robert's lists these cases:

- **The person who made the motion** gets to speak on it first, if they haven't spoken yet. The same goes for whoever presents a committee report, moves to take something from the table, or moves to reconsider.
- **Nobody speaks twice before everyone who wants to has spoken once.**
- **Alternate sides.** When the chair knows where people stand, they should go back and forth between those for and against.
- **Whoever set up the next step gets to take it.** If someone tabled a motion to deal with something urgent, they get the floor to bring up that urgent item. If someone moved to suspend the rules to make a motion, they get the floor to make it.

**If the chair isn't sure who was first,** they can let the group decide. Any two members can appeal the chair's choice of who gets the floor, one making the appeal and one seconding it.

**Once you have the floor, you keep it.** Calling out "Question!", moving to adjourn, or moving to table from your seat doesn't take the floor from you, and the chair should shut that down. The only things that can interrupt someone with the floor are:

- a motion to reconsider (made so it's on record in time)
- a point of order
- an objection to considering the motion
- a call to get back to the agenda when it isn't being followed
- a question of privilege
- a demand to split unrelated items into separate votes
- an urgent question about procedure or the facts

Even those shouldn't interrupt once the speaker's actually talking unless it really can't wait. And the speaker gets the floor back afterward.

## Watch out for

- **Motions shouted from a seat.** A motion made by someone who hasn't been recognized only counts if no one objects. If anybody else then asks for the floor, the chair should treat that motion as never made.
- **"Brother Moderator."** Robert's 1915 notes that "Brother Moderator" or "Brother Chairman" suggests the speaker is a fellow moderator or chairman, and says not to use it. Address the person by their title.
- **Handing a report to the secretary to read.** You don't lose the floor by doing that. Once it's read, the floor's still yours to make the motion to adopt it.

## At MBC

The Senior Pastor presides at church business meetings. If he's absent or disqualified, the Chairman of Deacons presides, and then the Vice-Chairman (Bylaws Art. II.A, II.D). That's who you address and who recognizes speakers. In Deacon Board meetings, it's the Chairman.
