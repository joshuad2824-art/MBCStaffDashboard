# Glossary — the terms that trip people up

**Short answer:** Plain-language meanings for the words Robert's uses, in alphabetical order. Each one links to the article that explains it in full.

**Adjourned meeting.** A continuation of an earlier meeting, held at a time the group set before it adjourned. It's part of the same session. See [Recess and adjourn](recess-and-adjourn.md).

**Amendment of the first and second degree.** A change to a motion is first degree. A change to that change is second degree. There's no third degree. See [Amend](amend.md).

**Appeal.** Asking the group to overrule the chair's ruling. See [Point of order and appeal](point-of-order-and-appeal.md).

**Assembly.** Robert's word for the group meeting, whether that's the congregation in a business meeting, the Deacon Board, or a committee.

**Ballot.** A secret written vote. See [Ways to vote](voting-methods.md).

**Blank.** A spot in a motion left open for a name, number, date, or amount, which the group fills by voting on suggestions in turn. See [Amend](amend.md).

**Call for the orders of the day.** A demand that the meeting get back to its agenda or take up scheduled business. See [Order of business](order-of-business.md).

**Call the question / Previous Question.** A motion to end discussion and vote now. It takes two-thirds. See [Call the question](previous-question.md).

**The chair.** Whoever's presiding, and how that person refers to themselves: "The chair rules…" See [Chairing a meeting](chairing-a-meeting.md).

**Commit / refer.** To send a motion to a committee. *Recommit* means sending it back to a committee a second time. See [Refer to a committee](refer-to-committee.md).

**Debate.** Formal discussion of a motion. A question or a brief suggestion doesn't count as debate. See [Debate rules](debate-rules.md).

**Dilatory.** Meant to stall. See [Stalling motions](dilatory-motions.md).

**Division of the assembly.** A demand to retake an unclear voice vote standing. See [Division of the assembly](division-of-the-assembly.md).

**Division of the question.** Splitting a motion into parts for separate votes. See [Divide the question](divide-the-question.md).

**Ex officio.** Being a member of a board or committee because of another office you hold. See [Committees and boards](committees-and-boards.md).

**The floor.** The right to speak at that moment. You "have the floor" once the chair recognizes you. See [Getting the floor](getting-the-floor.md).

**General consent.** Acting without a formal vote when nobody objects. Also called unanimous or silent consent. See [General consent](general-consent.md).

**General order.** Business postponed to a time or meeting. It's taken up when it's reached, but it can't interrupt anything. See [Postpone to a set time](postpone-to-a-set-time.md).

**Germane.** Related to the subject. Amendments have to be germane. See [Amend](amend.md).

**Immediately pending question.** The motion the group has to deal with right now, which is the last one the chair stated that's still pending. See [Motion, start to finish](how-a-motion-moves.md).

**Incidental motion.** A motion that arises out of the business at hand and has to be settled before it, like a point of order or a request to withdraw. See [Precedence ladder](precedence.md).

**Main motion.** A motion that brings new business before the group. Only one can be pending at a time. See [Main motion](main-motion.md).

**Majority.** More than half of the votes cast. Blanks and people who don't vote aren't counted. See [Majority vs. two-thirds](majority-and-two-thirds.md).

**Minutes.** The record of what the group *did*, not what was said. See [Secretary and minutes](secretary-and-minutes.md).

**Moderator.** Robert's notes this is the usual title for the presiding officer "especially in religious assemblies." At MBC, it's the title for whoever presides at business meetings.

**Notice.** Advance word, given at the previous meeting or in the call for this one, that a motion is coming. Some motions require it, or need a lower vote with it. See [Rescind](rescind.md) and [Amending the bylaws](amending-the-bylaws.md).

**Pending.** A motion is pending once the chair has stated it and until it's been decided or otherwise set aside.

**Plurality.** The most votes, but short of a majority. It never decides anything unless a rule says it does.

**Point of order.** Saying a rule is being broken. The chair rules on it. See [Point of order and appeal](point-of-order-and-appeal.md).

**Privileged motion.** An urgent motion that isn't about the business on the floor but can interrupt it: adjourning, recess, questions of privilege, and calls for the orders of the day. See [Precedence ladder](precedence.md).

**Put the question.** For the chair to take the vote. See [Motion, start to finish](how-a-motion-moves.md).

**Question.** Another word for a motion once it's in front of the group. "The question is on the amendment."

**Quorum.** The minimum number of members who have to be present to do business. See [Quorum](quorum.md).

**Ratify.** To approve after the fact something done without authorization. See [Ratify](ratify.md).

**Recognize.** For the chair to give a member the floor.

**Reconsider.** To take a decided vote up again, as if it never happened. See [Reconsider](reconsider.md).

**Rescind.** To cancel something adopted earlier. See [Rescind](rescind.md).

**Seriatim.** Paragraph by paragraph. See [Divide the question](divide-the-question.md).

**Session.** A meeting plus any adjourned continuations of it. For groups that meet regularly, each regular meeting is usually its own session. See [Meeting vs. session](meeting-vs-session.md).

**Sine die.** "Without a day." Adjourning without setting a time to meet again, which closes the session.

**Special order.** Business set for a specific time with priority, so it can interrupt other business when that time comes. Making one takes two-thirds. See [Postpone to a set time](postpone-to-a-set-time.md).

**Standing rule.** A practical rule a majority can adopt at any meeting. See [Bylaws and standing rules](bylaws-and-standing-rules.md).

**State the question.** For the chair to announce a motion to the group, which puts it in front of them. See [Motion, start to finish](how-a-motion-moves.md).

**Subsidiary motion.** A motion that acts on the pending motion, like amending, referring, postponing, tabling, or limiting or ending discussion. See [Precedence ladder](precedence.md).

**Suspend the rules.** To set aside a procedural rule for one stated purpose. It takes two-thirds. See [Suspend the rules](suspend-the-rules.md).

**Table / lay on the table.** To set a motion aside temporarily. See [Lay on the table](lay-on-the-table.md).

**Tellers.** The people who count the votes. See [Ways to vote](voting-methods.md).

**Two-thirds.** Two-thirds of the votes cast. See [Majority vs. two-thirds](majority-and-two-thirds.md).

**Unfinished business.** Business left over from the last meeting, taken up before new business. See [Order of business](order-of-business.md).

*Source: Robert's Rules of Order Revised (1915), Introduction (Definitions) and the sections cited above*
