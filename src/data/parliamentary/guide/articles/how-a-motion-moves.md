# How a motion gets from idea to vote

**Short answer:** Someone makes a motion, someone seconds it, and the chair states it to the group. Then the group discusses it, the chair puts it to a vote, and the chair announces the result. Nothing is actually before the group until the chair states it, and nothing is decided until the chair announces it.

## The six steps

1. **A member makes the motion.** They get the floor and say, "I move that…" (see [Getting the floor](getting-the-floor.md)). Anything long or important should be in writing, and the chair can require that.
2. **Someone seconds it.** "Second." A second just means at least one other person wants the group to take it up, not that they're for it (see [Seconding](seconding.md)).
3. **The chair states it.** "It's been moved and seconded that we… Are you ready for the question?" This is the step people skip, and it's the one that puts the motion in front of the group. Until then, the chair can still rule it out of order.
4. **The group discusses it.** Discussion has to stay on the motion that's on the floor (see [Debate](debate-rules.md)).
5. **The chair puts the question.** When the discussion winds down, the chair asks once more, "Are you ready for the question?" If nobody speaks up, the chair restates the motion and calls for the vote: "All in favor, say aye. Those opposed, say no."
6. **The chair announces the result.** "The ayes have it, and the motion is adopted," or, "The noes have it, and the motion is lost." Then the chair says what comes next.

## What the chair says, start to finish

> "It's been moved and seconded that we [motion]. Are you ready for the question?"
> …discussion…
> "Are you ready for the question? The question is on [motion]. All in favor, say aye. Those opposed, say no. The ayes have it, and the motion is adopted. The next item is…"

If the motion can't be discussed or amended, like a motion to adjourn or to table, the chair skips "Are you ready for the question?" and goes straight to the vote.

## How it works

**Before the chair states it, the motion still belongs to the person who made it.** People can suggest changes, and the mover can reword it or pull it entirely without asking anybody. If the mover changes it, the person who seconded it can take their second back. Once the chair states it, it belongs to the group, and changing or withdrawing it takes the group's permission (see [Withdrawing a motion](withdraw-and-requests.md)).

**Only one main motion can be on the floor at a time.** While it's there, other motions can be made that act on it, like amending it, referring it, or postponing it. Whatever the chair stated most recently is what the group's dealing with right now, and it gets voted on first. Robert calls that the "immediately pending question."

**The chair always says what's next.** After every vote, the chair names the next item or says what's now on the floor, like "The question is now on the motion as amended." That one habit prevents most of the confusion in a meeting.

**Routine things don't need a motion.** Receiving a report, reading a letter, approving the minutes: the chair can just handle these, and if someone objects, it goes to a regular motion and vote (see [General consent](general-consent.md)).

**Vote first by voice or show of hands.** The exception is anything that needs two-thirds. For those, Robert says to take a standing vote from the start so the count is clear.

## Watch out for

- **Discussing a motion before it's stated.** Talk before the chair states the motion should be short and about the wording, not a debate.
- **Forgetting to announce the vote.** The result doesn't take effect until the chair announces it. Robert treats the announcement as part of taking the vote.
- **Two motions at once.** A member can't make two motions at the same time unless no one objects. There are a few built-in exceptions, like moving to suspend the rules and adopt something in one breath.
