# Setting something aside for now — "Lay on the Table"

**Short answer:** Tabling sets a motion aside so the group can deal with something more pressing, and a simple majority can bring it back later. It isn't meant to kill a motion, and using it that way is one of the most common misuses of Robert's.

`Second: Yes · Debate: No · Amend: No · Vote: Majority · Interrupt a speaker: No`

**What to say:** "I move that we lay the question on the table." To bring it back: "I move that we take the question from the table."

## How it works

You can only table a motion that's on the floor right now. When the vote passes, that motion goes into the secretary's keeping exactly as it is, along with any amendments attached to it. There's no discussion on the motion to table. The chair just takes the vote.

You can't put a time on it. If someone says "I move we table this until next month," that's really a motion to postpone, and the chair should restate it that way (see [Postpone to a set time](postpone-to-a-set-time.md)).

To bring it back, someone moves to take it from the table. They can do that once some other business has been handled and nothing else is on the floor. It takes a majority, and the motion comes back exactly as it left, amendments and all.

## Watch out for

- **Using it to shut something down.** Tabling has no debate and only needs a majority, so it's tempting as a quick way to end a conversation. Robert calls that unfair. If the group really doesn't want to take something up, the fair tools are [Objecting to even discussing it](objection-to-consideration.md) (before discussion starts) or [Calling the question](previous-question.md) and then voting it down. Both need two-thirds.
- **Tabling a whole category, like "old business."** You can only table a motion that's actually pending. To skip ahead, the group can agree by [general consent](general-consent.md) or [suspend the rules](suspend-the-rules.md) (two-thirds).
- **Thinking it stays there forever.** It doesn't. A tabled motion can be taken up through the end of the next regular meeting. After that it's off the table, and anyone who wants it back brings it as a new motion (see [Bringing a motion back](bringing-it-back.md)).

## At MBC

Business meetings are quarterly (Bylaws Art. IV §2 ¶1), so a motion tabled in one business meeting is available through the next quarter's meeting. The Deacon Board meets monthly (Bylaws Art. II.B §3 ¶5), so for deacons that window is this month's meeting and next month's.
