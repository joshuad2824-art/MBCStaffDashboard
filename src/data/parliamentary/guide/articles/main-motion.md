# Making a main motion

**Short answer:** A main motion brings a new item of business before the group. It's how anything gets decided. You can only make one when nothing else is on the floor, and it passes by a simple majority.

`Second: Yes · Debate: Yes · Amend: Yes · Vote: Majority · Interrupt a speaker: No`

**What to say:** "I move that we [do this]." Say what you want done, not what you think about it. "I move that we buy a new copier for the church office, not to exceed $2,500," not "I move we look at the copier situation."

## How it works

**One at a time.** A main motion can't be made while another question is on the floor. Once it's on the floor, other motions can be made that act on it, like [amending](amend.md) it, [referring it to a committee](refer-to-committee.md), or [postponing](postpone-to-a-set-time.md) it.

**Put it in writing when it matters.** Robert's says every resolution should be written down, and the chair can require any main motion, amendment, or committee instruction to be in writing. If it's long, has numbers in it, or the exact wording will matter later, write it out and hand it to the secretary.

**The formal version is a resolution.** "Resolved, That…" is the same thing as "I move that…" written for the record. If you want to give the reasons, they go in a preamble, with each reason starting "Whereas." The preamble gets amended last, since changes to the resolution may mean the reasons need to change too.

Here's an illustration of the form:

> Whereas, Several of our classroom tables are broken; and
> Whereas, The fall term starts next month; therefore
> Resolved, That the Building & Grounds Committee obtain bids for replacements and report at the next meeting.

**Some motions that don't look like main motions are main motions.** Robert's lists adopting a committee's report, amending something already adopted, rescinding, ratifying, and setting a future meeting time when nothing else is pending. They're all handled like main motions.

## Watch out for

- **Motions that conflict with the bylaws.** They're out of order, and if one passes anyway, even unanimously, it's void. Change the bylaws first (see [Votes that don't count](votes-that-dont-count.md)).
- **Motions that contradict something already decided.** If the group already adopted something at this session, a conflicting motion is out of order. [Reconsider](reconsider.md) the earlier vote if it isn't too late, or [rescind](rescind.md) it, and then make the new motion. Robert's says the same goes for a conflict with something adopted at an earlier session: rescind the old one first.
- **Vague motions.** "I move we do something about the parking lot" leaves the group nothing to vote on. If you don't know the answer yet, move to send it to a committee with instructions to come back with a recommendation.
- **Two motions at once.** One member can't make two motions at a time unless no one objects.

## At MBC

Robert's is a general guide here "except for changes specifically identified by these by-laws" (Bylaws Art. IV §2 ¶1), so a main motion has to fit the bylaws and policies first. For a special called business meeting, the bylaws require the notice to state "the particular object" of the meeting (Art. IV §2 ¶4). The plain reading is that business at that meeting stays within that stated purpose. That's a reading of our bylaw. The 1915 Robert's text doesn't address it directly.
