# Majority vs. two-thirds — what it takes to pass

**Short answer:** Most motions need a majority, meaning more than half of the votes actually cast. A two-thirds vote means two-thirds of the votes cast. In both cases, blanks and people who don't vote aren't counted. Two-thirds is reserved for motions that override a member's rights or the group's rules.

## How to count

**Majority = more than half of the votes cast,** at a properly called meeting with a [quorum](quorum.md) present.

- 30 vote: 16 is a majority.
- 29 vote: 15 is a majority.
- A tie fails.

**Two-thirds = two-thirds of the votes cast.** The yes votes need to be at least twice the no votes.

- 30 vote: 20 is two-thirds.
- 31 vote: 21 is two-thirds.

**The wording in a bylaw matters.** Robert's warns not to mix these up, and his example shows why. Say 20 members are present, 14 of them vote, and the group has 70 members:

| The bylaw says… | Votes needed |
|---|---|
| two-thirds (of the votes cast) | 10 |
| two-thirds of the members present | 14 |
| two-thirds of the members | 47 |

**Not voting isn't neutral.** Since only votes cast count, abstaining doesn't add to either side. But when a rule is based on *members present*, someone who doesn't vote has the same effect as a no vote.

**A plurality never wins** unless a rule says it does. The most votes isn't enough without a majority, so if no candidate has a majority, vote again.

## Why some things take two-thirds

Robert's describes it as a compromise between the rights of the individual and the rights of the group. Two-thirds is required for any motion that:

- suspends or changes a rule of order already adopted
- keeps a question from being considered
- closes, limits, or extends discussion
- limits the freedom to nominate or vote, or closes nominations or the polls
- takes away someone's membership or office

## Motions that take two-thirds (Robert's default)

- [Amend or rescind](amending-the-bylaws.md) the constitution, bylaws, or rules of order. This also requires notice.
- [Amend or rescind](rescind.md) a standing rule, an order of business, or a resolution already adopted, without notice
- Take up a question out of its proper order
- [Suspend the rules](suspend-the-rules.md)
- [Make a special order](postpone-to-a-set-time.md)
- Refuse to proceed to the orders of the day, or extend the time set for adjourning or a recess
- [Sustain an objection to consideration](objection-to-consideration.md)
- [Call the question](previous-question.md)
- [Limit or extend discussion](limit-debate.md)
- [Close nominations](nominations-and-elections.md) or the polls, or limit the names to be voted for
- Expel a member or remove someone from office. Both also require notice.
- [Discharge a committee](refer-to-committee.md) without notice

## At MBC — thresholds set by our own documents

These override Robert's defaults.

| Decision | Vote required | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Call a Senior Pastor | **Three-fourths of those voting**, by secret ballot | Bylaws Art. II.A |
| Elect an associate pastoral staff member | **Three-fourths of those present**, by secret ballot, at a special called business meeting | Art. II.F |
| Elect a deacon to the Board | Affirmative **majority of those voting** | Art. II.B §3 ¶11 |
| Elect Deacon Board officers | **Simple majority** of the Board | Art. II.B §3 ¶3 |
| Elect a moderator (when the pastor, chairman, and vice-chairman are all unavailable) | **Majority of those present** | Art. II.D |
| Amend the bylaws | **Simple majority of members present and voting**, with a month's written notice | Art. V |
| Adopt the church budget | **Simple majority**, approve or oppose only, no amendments | Policy A009 ¶2.3 |
| Hold a meeting to consider ending the pastoral relationship | **Majority of qualified members present** | Art. IV §2 ¶5.4 |
| End the pastoral relationship, at that meeting | **At least a simple majority of qualified members present** | Art. IV §2 ¶5.4 |
| Terminate a membership for cause | Affirmative vote of **at least 75% of members present** at the hearing, by individual secret ballot | Art. I §4 ¶3 |
| Restore a terminated membership | **Majority of members present and voting** | Art. I §5 |
| Approve a deacon candidate for ordination | **Three-fourths of the Deacon Board** (bylaws); the DIT program text says a simple majority of the Deacon Body by secret ballot. See note below | Art. II.B §1; DIT Program ¶H.D |

Several of these are based on *members present*, not votes cast. In those cases, a member who's present and doesn't vote counts the same as a no vote.

**Ordination vote — the documents disagree.** The bylaws require a three-fourths affirmative vote of the Deacon Board (Art. II.B §1). The Deacon-in-Training program says a simple majority of the Deacon Body by secret ballot (DIT ¶H.D). The bylaws rank higher, so three-fourths controls until the two are reconciled. Using a secret ballot satisfies both. This one should be added to the discrepancy docket.

The Art. I §4 ¶3 wording is "the affirmative vote of a majority of at least 75% of the members present." The table reads that as 75% of those present.
