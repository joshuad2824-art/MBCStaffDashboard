# Meeting vs. session — why it matters

**Short answer:** For a group that meets regularly, like a church business meeting or a monthly board meeting, each regular meeting is its own session unless the group adjourns to a set time to keep going. That matters because a lot of rules run "for the rest of the session," like when a failed motion can come back or how long something stays on the table.

## How it works

**A meeting** is one sitting, with no break longer than a short recess.

**A session** is a meeting together with any continuations of it. If the group adjourns and sets a time to pick up where it left off, that next gathering is an "adjourned meeting" of the same session, not a new session. A session ends when the group adjourns without setting a time to continue.

**For groups that meet on a regular schedule,** Robert's treats each regular or called meeting as its own session unless the group has a rule that says otherwise.

**Why Robert's prefers short sessions.** Once a motion is adopted, rejected, or blocked from consideration, it can't come back in the same session. If one session lasted a whole year, a temporary majority at one meeting could lock the subject out for months.

## Rules that turn on "session"

- A motion that was voted down can't be brought back in the same session, but it can at the next one (see [Bringing a motion back](bringing-it-back.md)).
- A tabled motion stays available through the end of the next regular session (see [Lay on the table](lay-on-the-table.md)).
- You can't move to reconsider a vote from an earlier session. A motion to reconsider that was made at the last session can still be called up, though (see [Reconsider](reconsider.md)).
- A standing rule adopted at one session can be suspended for a later session by a majority vote.
- Postponing an item past the next session isn't allowed. It would keep the next session from dealing with it.

## Watch out for

- **"Quarterly meeting" or "annual meeting."** In these phrases, Robert's says "meeting" means the whole session, including any adjourned meetings that continue it. Business that has to happen at that meeting can happen at any point in that session.

## At MBC

Church business meetings are quarterly (Bylaws Art. IV §2 ¶1), and the Deacon Board meets monthly (Art. II.B §3 ¶5). So under Robert's default, each quarterly business meeting is its own session, and so is each monthly Board meeting. If either one adjourns to a set date to finish its work, that later gathering is part of the same session.
