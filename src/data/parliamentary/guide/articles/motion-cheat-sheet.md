# Motion cheat sheet

**Short answer:** Most motions follow the same five rules: they need a second, they can be discussed, they can be amended, they pass by a majority, and you can't interrupt someone to make one. This chart shows where each motion breaks from that pattern.

**How to read it.** *Second* means somebody has to second it before the chair takes it up. *Discuss* means the group can debate it. *Amend* means it can be changed. *Vote* is what it takes to pass. *Interrupt* means you can make it while someone else has the floor. "Majority" always means more than half of the votes actually cast, and "two-thirds" means two-thirds of the votes cast. Blanks and people who don't vote don't count either way.

## Notes

1. You can't discuss it if the motion it applies to can't be discussed.
2. You can amend an amendment, but you can't amend *that*. Two levels is the limit.
3. If the motion bundles together separate, unrelated items, any one member can require them to be split, even while someone else has the floor, and no second or vote is needed. If the parts are on the same subject, it takes a regular motion and a majority.
4. This one opens up the original motion for discussion too, not just the motion itself.
5. It has to be raised right when the motion is introduced, before any discussion starts. It takes a two-thirds vote *against* considering the motion to keep it off the floor.
6. You can *make* the motion while someone else has the floor so it gets on record in time, but the group doesn't take it up until the current business allows. Only someone who voted on the winning side can make it, and only on the day of the vote or the next day.
7. There's no discussion if the appeal is about rude or out-of-line conduct, about the rules of speaking, about which business comes first, or if it's made while a motion that can't be discussed is pending. When there is discussion, each member gets to speak once.
8. Before the chair states the motion to the group, the person who made it can withdraw or reword it freely. After that, it takes the group's permission.
9. There's no discussion if it's made while other business is on the floor. Made at any other time, it's handled like a regular main motion.

## At MBC

Robert's general rule (two-thirds with notice) only applies to bylaw amendments when the bylaws are silent, and ours aren't. MBC's bylaws can be amended by a simple majority of those present and voting. The proposal has to be laid before the church in writing and discussed at a business meeting at least a month ahead, it's read from the pulpit each Sunday after that (or published weekly), and it's included in the call for the meeting (Bylaws Art. V).
