# Nominations and elections

**Short answer:** Nominations put names forward, and they don't need a second. A nominating committee's slate is just a set of nominations, so there's no vote to "accept" it, and the floor can add more. To win, a candidate needs a majority of the votes cast, not just the most votes. An election takes effect right away if the person is there and doesn't decline.

## Nominations

**How they're made.** If the bylaws don't say, the group can decide by majority vote, without discussion, how nominations will be made. The options are:

- by the chair
- from the floor
- by a nominating committee
- by ballot
- by mail

**From the floor.** Say "I nominate…" No second is needed. The chair repeats each name. In a small meeting, people often just suggest names from their seats.

**From a committee.** When the nominating committee reports its slate, there's no vote on the report. The chair then asks, "Are there any further nominations?" and takes names from the floor. Committee nominees and floor nominees stand on equal footing.

**Closing nominations.** Before the election, the chair asks for any further nominations. If there aren't any, the chair declares nominations closed. A formal motion to close takes two-thirds, since it limits members' rights, and it isn't in order until there's been a reasonable chance to nominate. Reopening nominations takes a majority.

**Nominations aren't always required.** In a ballot or roll-call election, members can vote for any eligible person, whether nominated or not.

## Elections

**Voice or standing vote.** The chair takes the nominees one at a time, in the order nominated, and stops at the first one who gets a majority.

**Ballot.** Robert's calls this the usual method in permanent societies. Keep balloting until each office has a majority winner (see [Ways to vote](voting-methods.md)).

**Majority, not plurality.** The most votes isn't enough unless a rule says so (see [Majority vs. two-thirds](majority-and-two-thirds.md)).

**When it takes effect:**

- **Right away,** if the person is present and doesn't decline, or is absent but agreed to run.
- **If they're absent and didn't agree,** it takes effect when they're told, provided they don't decline right then.
- **After that, it can't be reconsidered.** Once the election has taken effect and the person knows, it's too late. Unless the rules set a start date, the new officer takes office immediately.

**Declining.** If you're elected to something you can't do, decline right away. If you stay silent, you've accepted (see [Withdraw and requests](withdraw-and-requests.md)).

## Watch out for

- **"Move to make it unanimous."** This is out of order when the bylaws require a ballot, unless the motion itself is voted on by ballot. A single no vote defeats it.
- **Having the secretary "cast one ballot."** It's out of order when a ballot is required, because it ends the secrecy that protects a dissenter.
- **Moving to accept the nominating committee's report.** There's nothing to accept. The names are nominations.

## At MBC

- **Church officers and committees.** The Nominating Committee finds, interviews, and nominates people for all positions, except where the bylaws or church action provide otherwise (Bylaws Art. III §3 ¶1).
- **Deacons.**
  - *Nominations* are made on the approved form at least 30 days before the business meeting, with nominations from the church received before nominations from the Board. The pastor and the Chairman of the Deacon Board screen each nominee for qualifications (Art. II.B §3 ¶10).
  - *The election* is held at the business meeting before the September Board meeting (¶9).
  - *To be elected* takes an affirmative majority of those voting (¶11).
  - *Vacancies in unexpired terms* are nominated by the Board (¶9).
- **Deacon Board officers.** The Board elects its own Chairman, Vice-Chairman, and Secretary each year by simple majority (¶3).
- **Pastoral staff.** These elections have their own rules, including secret ballots and three-fourths thresholds (Art. II.A, II.F). See [Majority vs. two-thirds](majority-and-two-thirds.md).
