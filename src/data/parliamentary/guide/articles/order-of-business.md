# Order of business — the agenda, and getting back to it

**Short answer:** If the group hasn't adopted its own agenda, Robert's gives a standard one: minutes, reports, special orders, unfinished business, new business. To take something out of order, the group needs everyone's agreement or a two-thirds vote. Any one member can call the meeting back to the agenda when it drifts.

## Robert's standard order

1. **Read and approve the minutes** of the last meeting.
2. **Reports of boards, standing committees, and officers** who are required to report.
3. **Reports of special committees** (ones formed for a single task).
4. **Special orders**: items the group voted to take up at this meeting with priority (see [Postpone to a set time](postpone-to-a-set-time.md)).
5. **Unfinished business and general orders.** This is what people usually call "old business." It goes in this order: whatever was on the floor when the last meeting adjourned, then items that were scheduled for the last meeting but never reached, then items postponed to this meeting.
6. **New business.**

The secretary should give the chair a written list of everything that's coming up. The chair announces each item as the last one wraps up, which keeps the meeting moving.

## How it works

**Taking something out of order.** There are three ways to do it:

- Ask for general consent: "If there's no objection, we'll take up the building report first." If one person objects, it takes one of the other two ways.
- Suspend the rules, which takes a two-thirds vote (see [Suspend the rules](suspend-the-rules.md)).
- Lay the item in front of the group on the table, by majority, to clear the way. This only works on the one motion actually being considered.

Robert's also mentions "passing" an item by general consent. The group skips it for now, and it comes back up as soon as the item it was skipped for is done.

**Getting back on track: Call for the Orders of the Day.** When the meeting has drifted from the agenda, or a scheduled item's time has come and the chair hasn't brought it up, any one member can say, "I call for the orders of the day." It needs no second and can interrupt a speaker. The chair then either moves to the scheduled business or asks, "Will the assembly proceed to the orders of the day?" It takes a two-thirds vote *against* to keep going with what's on the floor instead.

**Needing more time on the current item.** When it's time for the next scheduled item, someone can move to extend the time on the current one by a set number of minutes. That takes two-thirds.

## Watch out for

- **Tabling a whole category.** You can't table or postpone "the reports" or "old business" as a group. Only the one motion on the floor can be tabled or postponed.
- **Calling for the orders of the day when nothing's out of order.** It only works when the meeting has actually strayed from the agenda.
- **Reading the minutes more than once.** Robert's has the minutes read once a day, at the start of business.

## At MBC

The bylaws and policies don't set an order of business for church business meetings or Deacon Board meetings. That means Robert's standard order applies unless the body has adopted its own. One item is fixed: the Treasurer reports receipts and disbursements to the deacons at each regular monthly meeting, and that report also goes to the church at its regular business meeting (Bylaws Art. II.C ¶2). In Robert's order, it falls under step 2.
