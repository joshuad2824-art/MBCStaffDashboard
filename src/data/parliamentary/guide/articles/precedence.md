# What outranks what — the order of precedence

**Short answer:** Motions stack. While one is pending, you can make any motion *above* it on the ladder below, but not one below it. The group then votes from the top down, settling the highest-ranking motion first.

## The ladder

Highest at the top. When a motion is pending, the ones above it are in order. The ones below it aren't.

| Rank | Motion | Discuss? | Amend? | Vote |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [Set the time to adjourn to](recess-and-adjourn.md) | No | Yes | Majority |
| 2 | [Adjourn](recess-and-adjourn.md) | No | No | Majority |
| 3 | [Take a recess](recess-and-adjourn.md) | No | Yes | Majority |
| 4 | [Raise a question of privilege](questions-of-privilege.md) | No | No | Chair rules |
| 5 | [Call for the orders of the day](order-of-business.md) | No | No | None needed |
| 6 | [Lay on the table](lay-on-the-table.md) | No | No | Majority |
| 7 | [Call the question](previous-question.md) (Previous Question) | No | No | **Two-thirds** |
| 8 | [Limit or extend discussion](limit-debate.md) | No | Yes | **Two-thirds** |
| 9 | [Postpone to a set time](postpone-to-a-set-time.md) | Yes | Yes | Majority |
| 10 | [Refer to a committee](refer-to-committee.md) | Yes | Yes | Majority |
| 11 | [Amend](amend.md) | Yes | Yes | Majority |
| 12 | [Postpone indefinitely](postpone-indefinitely.md) | Yes | No | Majority |
| 13 | [Main motion](main-motion.md) | Yes | Yes | Majority |

Ranks 1–5 are **privileged motions.** They aren't about the business on the floor, but they're urgent enough to interrupt it. Ranks 1 and 3 are only privileged when other business is pending. Setting the time to adjourn to is also only privileged when no other meeting is already scheduled for the same day or the next. Otherwise they're ordinary main motions. Adjourning loses its privilege if it's qualified in any way.

Ranks 6–12 are **subsidiary motions.** They act on the motion that's pending, to change it, delay it, or dispose of it.

## The motions that float

**Incidental motions** don't have a fixed rank. They arise out of whatever's pending and have to be settled before it. These include:

- [points of order and appeals](point-of-order-and-appeal.md)
- [suspending the rules](suspend-the-rules.md)
- [objecting to consideration](objection-to-consideration.md)
- [dividing the question](divide-the-question.md)
- [calls for a counted vote](division-of-the-assembly.md)
- [inquiries and requests](withdraw-and-requests.md)

**Reconsider** is unusual. You can *make* it almost any time, even while someone has the floor, but it's *taken up* at the rank of the motion it would reconsider (see [Reconsider](reconsider.md)).

## A worked example

1. A main motion is on the floor: "Buy new hymnals." (13)
2. Someone moves to amend it: "…not to exceed $3,000." (11, in order because it's above 13)
3. Someone else moves to refer the whole thing to the Finance Committee. (10, in order because it's above 11)
4. Someone moves to postpone indefinitely. **Out of order.** That's 12, below the motion to refer that's pending.
5. Someone calls the question on the motion to refer. (7, in order)

The group votes from the top down:

- **First, on calling the question.** If it passes, the group votes on the motion to refer, with no more discussion. If it fails, discussion of the motion to refer continues.
- **Next, on the motion to refer.** If it passes, the main motion and its pending amendment go to the committee together. If it fails, the group goes back to the amendment.
- **Then the amendment, then the main motion.**

## Watch out for

- **Not knowing what's pending.** After every vote, the chair should say what's now on the floor. Robert's calls neglecting this "one of the most prolific causes of confusion."
- **Ranking isn't value.** A higher-ranking motion isn't more important. It's just more urgent or more immediate, so it gets settled first.
