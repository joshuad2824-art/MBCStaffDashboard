# Ending discussion — "Call the question" (the Previous Question)

**Short answer:** Calling the question is a motion to stop discussion and vote right now. It needs a second and a two-thirds vote, and it can't be discussed itself. Saying "Question!" from your seat doesn't end anything. Only the vote does.

`Second: Yes · Debate: No · Amend: No · Vote: Two-thirds · Interrupt a speaker: No`

**What to say:** Get the floor, then: "I move the previous question," or "I call the question." If several motions are pending, say which ones: "I move the previous question on the amendment and the main motion."

## How it works

**It takes two-thirds because it overrides a basic right.** Every member has the right to have a question fully discussed before it's decided. Robert's says cutting that off requires two-thirds.

**The chair takes the vote immediately,** and Robert's has it done as a standing vote:

> "The previous question is moved on [the motion]. Those in favor of ordering the previous question, please stand. Be seated. Those opposed, please stand. Be seated. There are two-thirds in favor, and the previous question is ordered. The question is now on [the motion]. All in favor…"

**If it passes,** discussion is over. Nobody can amend, and no other motion can be applied except tabling. The chair puts each motion it covers to a vote, in order.

**If it fails,** discussion simply picks up where it left off, as if the motion had never been made.

**It covers only what you name.** If you don't specify, it applies only to the motion immediately in front of the group, which is often just an amendment. To end discussion on everything pending, say so.

**It ends with the session.** If any of the motions it covers carries over to a later meeting, it's open for discussion again.

**Undoing it.** It can be reconsidered only before any vote has been taken under it.

## Watch out for

- **Treating "Question!" as an order to vote.** A member calling out "Question!" while someone else has the floor is out of order, and the chair should protect the speaker. Without the motion and a two-thirds vote, the chair goes on with discussion.
- **Calling it from your seat without the floor.** It's a motion like any other. Get recognized first.
- **Using it too early.** It's legitimate whether you want to pass or kill the motion. But Robert's notes that in ordinary societies, harmony matters enough that a group shouldn't be forced to a final vote without free discussion unless two-thirds want it.
- **A close vote on it.** If there's any doubt about the count, the chair should take the vote again right away and count each side.
