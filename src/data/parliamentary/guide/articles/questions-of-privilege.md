# Questions of privilege — when something in the room needs fixing now

**Short answer:** A question of privilege is how you raise an urgent problem with the meeting itself or with your standing as a member, like people in the back not being able to hear. Only adjourning and recess outrank it. It can interrupt a speaker if it truly can't wait, and the chair decides whether it qualifies.

`Second: No · Debate: No (to raise it) · Amend: No · Vote: None — the chair decides if it's a question of privilege · Interrupt a speaker: Only if urgent`

**What to say:** "Mr. Chairman, I rise to a question of privilege affecting the assembly," or "…to a question of personal privilege." Don't wait to be recognized. When the chair asks, state it: "The people in the back can't hear the report."

## How it works

**The chair decides two things:** whether it's really a question of privilege, and whether it's urgent enough to interrupt. Any two members can appeal that decision. If it qualifies but isn't urgent, the speaker finishes first, and then the chair gives you the floor.

**Many are fixed on the spot.** If it needs a decision, you make a motion, and that motion is handled like any main motion. It can be discussed, amended, and so on.

**Then business resumes exactly where it stopped.** If someone had the floor, the chair gives it back to them.

**It can't interrupt voting** or the checking of a vote.

## What counts

**Privileges of the assembly,** which take priority if two compete, are about:

- how the group's organized
- the comfort of the room: heat, lighting, ventilation, noise, disturbances
- the conduct of officers or employees
- disciplining a member for disorderly conduct
- the accuracy of published reports of the meeting

**Personal privilege** has to concern you as a member. It covers things like charges against your character that would, if true, unfit you for membership. It isn't for a general complaint or for answering an argument.

## Watch out for

- **"Point of personal privilege" to make a speech.** If it isn't about your rights as a member, it's not a question of privilege. Ask for the floor in the usual way.
- **Privileged questions vs. questions of privilege.** "Privileged questions" is a broader category. It also includes adjourning, recess, and calling for the orders of the day. A "question of privilege" is just the one described here.
