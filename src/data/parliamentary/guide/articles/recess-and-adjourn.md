# Taking a break, and ending the meeting — recess and adjourn

**Short answer:** A motion to adjourn ends the meeting. A recess is a short break. If made while other business is on the floor, both take a majority with no discussion. The meeting isn't over until the chair says it is, so everyone stays seated until then.

## Adjourn

`Second: Yes · Debate: No · Amend: No · Vote: Majority · Interrupt a speaker: No`

**What to say:** "I move that we adjourn."

- **It outranks almost everything.** Only setting the time to adjourn to ranks higher. Like any motion, though, you need the floor to make it.
- **The chair should check before putting it.** Make sure nothing important has been missed, and get announcements in. If something needs action first, the chair can say so and ask the mover to withdraw. Being undebatable doesn't stop the chair from telling the group what's still pending.
- **It can be made again** after any progress in business or discussion. The chair should refuse it when it's plainly being used to obstruct.
- **It can't be made during a vote.** It can be made after a ballot vote is taken but before it's announced. If so, the ballot result is announced first thing when the group meets again.
- **If it's qualified in any way,** like "adjourn at 9," or if adjourning would dissolve the group with no future meeting, it's a regular main motion that can be discussed and amended.

**What happens to unfinished business:**

- **If the group adjourned to a set time to keep going,** the interrupted business comes first after the minutes at that next meeting.
- **If the meeting closes the session, in a group that meets at least quarterly,** unfinished business is taken up at the next regular meeting where it left off, before new business.
- **In a body whose members serve set terms,** unfinished business falls to the ground when a term ends.

## Set the time to adjourn to

`Second: Yes · Debate: No (when other business is pending) · Amend: Yes · Vote: Majority · Interrupt a speaker: No`

**What to say:** "I move that when we adjourn, we adjourn to meet next Tuesday at 7 p.m."

This sets up a continuation of the same session, so the group can finish its work without waiting for the next regular meeting. It can even be made after the group has voted to adjourn, as long as the chair hasn't declared the meeting adjourned. The time can't be later than the next regular meeting.

## Recess

`Second: Yes · Debate: No (when other business is pending) · Amend: Yes, the length · Vote: Majority · Interrupt a speaker: No`

**What to say:** "I move that we recess for ten minutes."

It takes effect right away. When the time's up, the chair calls the meeting back to order and business picks up where it stopped. If a vote was taken but not yet announced before the break, the announcement comes first. Robert's mentions counting ballots as a good reason for a recess. Pushing back a scheduled recess or adjournment time takes two-thirds.

## Watch out for

- **Leaving before it's declared.** Members shouldn't leave their seats until the chair declares the meeting adjourned.
- **A motion to adjourn from someone without the floor.** Unless no one objects, the chair shouldn't take it.

## At MBC

**Deacon Board terms.** Board members serve three-year terms (Bylaws Art. II.B §3 ¶2), and new deacons replace those whose terms end on the last day of September (Art. II.B §3 ¶9). Under Robert's rule for bodies with members elected for set terms, business the Board leaves unfinished when a term expires falls to the ground. That means the September meeting. Anything still pending then would need to be brought up fresh in October. This is a reading of Robert's default applied to our Board, not a written MBC rule.

**Church business meetings.** These are quarterly (Art. IV §2 ¶1). Unfinished business carries to the next quarterly meeting, unless the church sets an adjourned meeting to finish sooner.
