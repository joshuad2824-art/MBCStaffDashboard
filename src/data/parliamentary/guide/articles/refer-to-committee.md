# Sending it to a committee — "Commit" or "Refer"

**Short answer:** Referring a motion hands it to a smaller group to study, fix, or act on, and then report back. It's the right move when a motion needs more information or more work than the room can do in one sitting. It takes a majority.

`Second: Yes · Debate: Yes, but only on whether to refer it · Amend: Yes · Vote: Majority · Interrupt a speaker: No`

**What to say:**

- "I move to refer the motion to the Finance Committee."
- "I move to refer the motion to a committee of three, appointed by the chair, to report at the next meeting."
- "I move to refer the motion to the Building & Grounds Committee with power to act."

## How it works

**Discussion stays on whether to refer it.** While this motion is pending, people can talk about whether sending it to a committee makes sense, but not about the merits of the motion itself.

**Pending amendments go with it.** If amendments were pending on the motion, they go to the committee along with it.

**Fill in the details.** A full motion to refer says:

- **which committee**
- **how many members**, if it's a new committee
- **how they're chosen**
- **any instructions**, like what the group wants back and when

If someone just says "refer it to a committee," the chair fills in the gaps one question at a time, starting with "To what committee?" When several committees are suggested, Robert's has them voted on in a set order, existing standing committees before a new one, and stops at the first that gets a majority.

**Choosing members for a new committee.** If several methods are suggested, they're voted on in this order:

1. ballot
2. nominations from the floor
3. nominations by the chair
4. appointment by the chair

When the chair appoints, there's no vote, but the chair has to announce the names before the committee can act. Whoever appoints the committee can also name its chairman and fill vacancies. If no chairman is named, the first person named serves as chairman unless the committee elects its own.

**Instructions can come later.** The group can give the committee instructions by majority vote at any time before it reports, even at a later meeting.

**"With power."** A committee given power to act can do the thing itself instead of coming back with a recommendation. When it's done, it reports what it did, and then the committee is finished.

## Who should be on it

- **A committee that's investigating a question** should include people from both sides, so the whole group can trust its report.
- **A committee that's carrying out a decision the group has already made** should be small and made up of people who are for it. If someone on it isn't in sympathy with the task, they should say so and ask to be excused.
- **The person who moved to refer** doesn't have to be on the committee. But Robert's says it's usual and courteous to include them when they're especially interested or informed.

## Taking it back

- **If the committee hasn't started yet**, the group can [reconsider](reconsider.md) the vote to refer on the same day or the next. That takes a majority.
- **After that**, the group has to vote to *discharge* the committee. That takes two-thirds, a majority of the whole membership, or a majority if notice was given ahead of time. It can be combined with taking the item up again: "I move that the committee be discharged and that the motion be taken up now."
- **While the committee has it**, the group can't take up anything that's essentially the same question.

## Watch out for

- **Referring to kill a motion.** If it's clear a referral will fail, Robert's notes that opponents sometimes call the question on it right away rather than spend time on the details.
- **Postpone indefinitely gets dropped.** If a motion to postpone indefinitely was pending, it doesn't go to the committee. It's simply dropped.

## At MBC

The natural places to refer something are the standing committees the bylaws already set up:

- the Deacon Board's standing committees, such as Finance, Personnel, and Building & Grounds (Bylaws Art. II.B §3)
- the church's standing committees (Art. III §3)

The bylaws also say how those committees' chairpersons are chosen, so for those committees, Robert's default about the first-named member serving as chairman doesn't apply.
