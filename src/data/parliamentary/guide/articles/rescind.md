# Canceling something already decided — "Rescind" or "Repeal"

**Short answer:** Rescind cancels something the group adopted at an earlier time. It takes a majority if notice was given at the previous meeting or in the call for this one. Without notice, it takes two-thirds, or a majority of the entire membership. Anyone can move it, no matter how they voted.

`Second: Yes · Debate: Yes, and the original action is open too · Amend: Yes · Vote: Majority with notice; two-thirds (or majority of all members) without · Interrupt a speaker: No`

**What to say:** "I move to rescind the motion adopted on [date] to [action]." To give notice for next time: "I'd like to give notice that at the next meeting I'll move to rescind the motion adopted on [date] to [action]."

## How it works

**It's a main motion.** It can only be made when nothing else is on the floor, and all the usual motions can be applied to it.

**It's the same as amending something already adopted.** Rescinding is basically striking out the entire action. Amending part of a past decision follows the same rules about notice and vote.

**Notice can be given while other business is pending,** but it can't interrupt a speaker.

**Reconsider first, if you still can.** If a motion to reconsider the vote has already been made and can still be called up, use that. Rescind isn't in order in that case.

## What can't be rescinded

- **Something that's already been done** and can't be undone.
- **A contract**, once the other party has been told.
- **An accepted resignation, an election, or an expulsion,** once the person was present or was officially notified. The only way to reverse an expulsion is to restore the person through the same steps as an election.

## Watch out for

- **Rescind vs. reconsider.**
  - *Reconsider* is for right after the vote, and only someone from the winning side can move it.
  - *Rescind* is for later, and anyone can move it.
- **Rules that require notice.** Rescinding or amending anything whose own rules require notice and a particular vote, like the bylaws, takes that same notice and vote.
- **"Expunging" from the record.** Rescinding doesn't erase the minutes. Robert's describes a rarer step where a group rescinds something and also marks it "Expunged by order of the assembly" in the record, with the words left readable. He adds that anything less than a majority of the total membership can't expunge an accurate record that's already been approved, even with a unanimous vote.

## At MBC

- **Bylaws.** Changing or repealing anything in the bylaws follows the bylaws' own process: written proposal, discussion at a business meeting at least a month ahead, pulpit or weekly publication, and inclusion in the call, then a simple majority of those present and voting (Bylaws Art. V). See [Amending the bylaws](amending-the-bylaws.md).
- **Other past actions of the church.** "Notice in the call for the meeting" fits naturally with how business meetings are already announced: from the pulpit and in the bulletin at least two days ahead (Art. IV §2 ¶1). Including a planned motion to rescind in that announcement is a reading of how Robert's notice rule would work here, not a written MBC rule.
