# The secretary and the minutes

**Short answer:** Minutes record what the group *did*, not what people *said*. Every main motion and what happened to it, any points of order and appeals, and the key facts of the meeting all go in. Opinions and summaries of the debate don't. Minutes are approved at the next meeting, usually by general consent, and can be corrected whenever an error turns up.

## What goes in the minutes

- **The kind of meeting:** regular, special, or adjourned
- **The name of the body,** and the date and place
- **Who presided and who kept the record,** or the names of any substitutes
- **Whether the last minutes were approved** or their reading was put off
- **Every main motion** that wasn't withdrawn, with its result, and who made it. The person who seconded isn't recorded.
- **Points of order and appeals,** whether upheld or not
- **Other motions that passed**
- **For counted votes and ballots,** the numbers on each side. For roll calls, the names on each side.
- **Adopted resolutions,** written out in full as adopted
- **The times** the meeting started and adjourned

**Leave out** what people said. Robert's says the secretary should record "what was done and not what was said," "never making criticisms, favorable or otherwise." If a report is important, the group can order it entered in the minutes in full.

> At a regular meeting of the Deacon Board, held October 12 in the fellowship hall, the Chairman presiding and the Secretary present, the minutes of the September meeting were read and approved. The Treasurer presented the monthly report… On motion of [member], the Board voted to… The meeting adjourned at 8:40 p.m.

## Approving and correcting

- **Read the minutes at the start of each regular meeting,** then correct and approve them, usually by general consent: "Are there any corrections? … There being none, the minutes stand approved."
- **Corrections** are usually made informally as people point them out. If anyone objects to a correction, it goes to a vote.
- **Errors can be fixed any time,** even long after approval. Once it's too late to reconsider the approval, a correction takes two-thirds, or a majority if notice was given.
- **Dispensing with the reading** takes a majority with no discussion. The minutes then come up later when nothing's pending, or at the next meeting before that meeting's own minutes are read.
- **If the next meeting is months away,** approve the minutes before adjourning, or authorize a committee to approve them.

## The secretary's other duties

- **Keep the records,** which members can inspect at reasonable times. A board's or committee's records are open to its own members.
- **Keep the roll of members** and call it when needed.
- **Notify people** of their election or appointment, and give committees the papers referred to them.
- **Send notices** of called meetings.
- **Prepare an order of business** for the chair before each meeting, and have the bylaws, minutes, and committee lists on hand.
- **Note on each committee report** the date it was received and what was done with it. No vote is needed to "place it on file."
- **Call the meeting to order** if the chair and vice chair are both absent, and preside until a chairman pro tem is elected.

## At MBC

**Church Clerk.** For the church, the bylaws give these duties to the Church Clerk (Bylaws Art. II.E). The Clerk:

- keeps the record of all church actions
- oversees the membership register, and the record of baptisms, admissions, dismissals, and deaths
- notifies officers, committee members, and delegates of their election
- issues letters of dismissal
- keeps official reports on file
- gives legal notice of meetings where the bylaws require it

**Deacon Board Secretary.** The Board has a Secretary among its officers (Art. II.B §3 ¶3). The bylaws give him the duty of serving written notice when the deacons recommend ending the pastoral relationship (Art. IV §2 ¶5.4). They don't separately say who keeps the Board's minutes.
