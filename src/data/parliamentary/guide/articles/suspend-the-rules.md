# Suspending the rules

**Short answer:** When the group wants to do something its procedural rules don't normally allow, like taking an item out of order or adopting something without discussion, it can suspend the rules for that one purpose. That takes two-thirds. It can't be used to set aside the bylaws, or any rule that protects absent members or the secrecy of a ballot.

`Second: Yes · Debate: No · Amend: No · Vote: Two-thirds · Interrupt a speaker: No`

**What to say:** Name the purpose:

- "I move to suspend the rules and take up the building report now."
- "I move to suspend the rules and adopt the following resolution…"

## How it works

**It covers one thing.** You have to say what the suspension is for, and nothing else can be done under it. The person who moved to suspend the rules gets the floor to make the motion it was for.

**When:** any time nothing else is pending, or while something is pending if the suspension is connected to it.

**What can be suspended:** rules about the order of business, about how business is conducted, and about who can attend. Those are generally rules of order. A standing rule, in the strict sense of one that isn't about procedure, can be suspended by a majority.

**Usually it's done by general consent.** "If there's no objection, we'll take up the youth trip report ahead of old business." A formal motion is only needed when someone objects.

**One try per meeting.** If a motion to suspend the rules for a purpose fails, it can't be renewed for the same purpose at that meeting unless everyone agrees. It can be tried again at another meeting, even on the same day.

## What it can't do

- **Set aside the bylaws or constitution,** unless the bylaws themselves provide for it. Some procedural rules that happen to sit in the bylaws can be suspended like ordinary rules of order.
- **Waive a rule that protects absent members,** like required notice. Not even by a unanimous vote.
- **Waive a ballot requirement.** Not even unanimously, and not by having one person cast the ballot for the group. Either would expose a dissenter's vote.
- **Override a large enough minority.** No rule can be suspended when the no votes are as large as the minority that rule was written to protect.
- **Go below the vote required to change the rule.** Anything that needs notice and two-thirds to amend can't be suspended by less than two-thirds.

## At MBC

These can't be suspended at a business meeting, even unanimously:

- **The notice process for bylaw amendments** (Bylaws Art. V)
- **The secret-ballot requirement** for calling a Senior Pastor (Art. II.A)
- **The secret-ballot requirement** for associate pastoral staff (Art. II.F)
- **The secret-ballot requirement** for absentee votes under Policy B001
