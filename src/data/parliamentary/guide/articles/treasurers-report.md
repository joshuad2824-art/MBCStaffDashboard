# The treasurer's report

**Short answer:** A treasurer's report is information. The group doesn't need to vote on it. Voting to "accept" it doesn't vouch for the figures anyway. Only an audit does that. The annual report goes to auditors, and adopting *their* report is what approves the treasurer's accounts.

## How it works

**No motion or vote is needed.** When the treasurer gives a report, Robert's says the chair should simply announce, without a motion, that it's referred to the auditors. If there aren't any auditors, the proper motion is to refer it to an auditing committee.

**Accepting a report doesn't check the math.** Voting to accept a financial report doesn't endorse the accuracy of the figures. The group can only be sure of those by having the report audited.

**The audit closes the loop.** The auditors examine the report and the receipts, then report back. When the group adopts the auditors' report, that approves the treasurer's report. It also relieves the treasurer of responsibility for any lost receipts, except in a case of fraud.

**Keep it readable.** Robert's says a financial report "is made for the information of members." Dates and individual payments for the same purpose get in the way. Checking those details is the auditors' job. A clear report shows the balance at the start, receipts by category, disbursements by category, and the balance at the end.

## Watch out for

- **"I move we accept the treasurer's report."** It's common, but it's not needed for a report that's only information, and it doesn't approve the figures. If the group wants a record, the minutes can simply note that the report was given.
- **Confusing the treasurer's report with the budget.** Adopting a budget is a main motion that commits the group. A treasurer's report just tells the group where things stand.

## At MBC

**Who the Treasurer is.** The Chairman of the Finance Committee serves as Treasurer of the Church (Bylaws Art. II.C).

**What the bylaws require:**

- **Monthly.** An itemized report of the prior month's receipts and disbursements goes to the deacons at each regular monthly meeting. The same report goes to the church at its regular business meeting (Art. II.C ¶2).
- **Annually.** Within 60 days after the fiscal year ends, an annual report goes to the deacons and the church, with total receipts and itemized disbursements (Art. II.C ¶3).
- **Audit.** The church's three-member Audit Committee audits the annual report before it's rendered (Art. II.C ¶4).
- **Approval and keeping.** After the Audit Committee approves the annual account and the church accepts it, the Treasurer delivers it to the Church Clerk for the permanent records (Art. II.C ¶5).
- **Open records.** The Treasurer's records, except contribution records, are open to any member's inspection at all times, and they don't leave the church (Art. II.C ¶4).

That's the same pattern Robert's describes: regular reports for information, and an audited annual report that the church accepts.
